How a caller that does not wait invokes the servitor, and what it survives

A picker, a launcher, a bar of the desktop or any other lesser machine may
invoke a rite and go its way without waiting for the verdict: the vox tells
the faithful how the invocation ended. Such a caller launches the servitor
detached, as the launcher fuzzel launches its programs — it forks, its child
execs the servitor with stdin upon /dev/null, and the errno of an exec that
fell is sent back through a pipe closed upon exec, so that the caller knows
at once whether the servitor awoke, and may then depart:

  - stdin, stdout and stderr upon /dev/null, never a pipe that the caller
    reads and then abandons;
  - a session of its own (setsid), so that no hangup of the caller's
    terminal reaches the servitor;
  - should the caller dwell in a cgroup or scope that may be slain whole,
    a launch prefix that sets the servitor in a scope of its own:
    systemd-run --user --scope … or uwsm app -- ….

Once launched, the servitor needs its caller no more. The death of the one
who launched it never slays it: the orphan is adopted by the machine and
performs its liturgy to the end. The servitor never detaches itself; that is
the caller's office.

The servitor stands steadfast besides, so that not even a careless caller
leaves a rite half performed. For the whole of an invocation, from the
ritual invoke as from the cogitator, it heeds no hangup, for a caller
departing is no command to undo. A word it speaks upon a stdout or stderr
whose reader is gone is lost in silence: it never touches the liturgy, its
reversion, the data-slate or the chronicle, and the death-mark still tells
the verdict. An interrupt (Ctrl-C) and SIGTERM are heeded as ever: they halt
the invocation and revert it. A cogitator whose terminal hangs up while it
performs a rite ends once the rite has ended, and never before.

Two blows no steadfastness survives. SIGKILL, which no process may refuse,
slays the servitor where it stands: the rite is left as it was mid-liturgy,
neither reverted nor chronicled, and the augury may find it corrupted until
it is invoked anew. And a caller whose whole cgroup or scope is slain takes
the servitor with it, unless a launch prefix set it apart.

Kindred passages: invoke, cogitator, vox, reversion, death-marks, binharic

Exempla:
  setsid -f servitor invoke theme porpl </dev/null >/dev/null 2>&1

  systemd-run --user --scope -- \
    setsid -f servitor invoke theme porpl </dev/null >/dev/null 2>&1

  uwsm app -- servitor invoke screenshot --region full </dev/null >/dev/null 2>&1
