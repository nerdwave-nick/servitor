How an invocation holds dominion over its rite and its vessels, and how another awaits it

Every invocation holds dominion over the rite it performs, and over every
vessel and tethered name its liturgy writes, from before its pre-flight to
its verdict: the ritual invoke as the cogitator, a rite as an edict. It
seizes the dominion before it reads the former aspect and passes the
pre-flight, and relinquishes it only once the liturgy has triumphed or its
reversion is done and the verdict is kept. Meanwhile no other invocation of
that rite performs: two invocations of one rite never interleave their
liturgies, and the later is performed after the earlier, so that the aspect
it commands is the one that remains.

Nor does another invocation write those vessels meanwhile. Several rites
may keep sanctums of different wards in one vessel; while one of them holds
dominion over it, the others wait. So no rite rewrites the vessel upon what
it read before another wrote it, and no reversion casts out the sanctum of
another rite. A vessel is known by the place where it truly dwells: reached
through a tether, or through a tethered hall, it is one with the same vessel
named directly, and a vessel the invocation is yet to consecrate is guarded
all the same, reckoned through the hall that is to hold it. The vessels are
those the liturgy writes as it is illuminated for this very invocation; a
vessel only the pre-flight reveals, named by the former aspect or reached
through a tether bound meanwhile, is seized before anything is performed.
An edict writes no vessel and holds dominion over its own name alone.

The dominion over the rite is seized first, then that over the vessels one
after another in the order of their paths, the same order for every
invocation; so no two invocations ever each hold what the other awaits.
Rites that share no vessel and no tethered name do not wait upon one
another; their invocations are performed side by side.

An invocation that finds any of its dominion held does not refuse. It tells
once that it waits, however much it awaits, then waits for the dominion to
be relinquished; no patience bounds the waiting, for the holder is bounded
by the patience of its own words. Upon a terminal the awaiting is one line
before the header (upon stderr under --binharic, withheld by --silence);
without a terminal it is the invocation's one desktop missive, as the vox
commands, replaced in place by the vox-casts that follow. Dominion free at
once is never spoken of. An interrupt (Ctrl-C) or SIGTERM ends the waiting,
and nothing is touched.

A rite that exalts anything is never performed without dominion: should its
dominion not be held at all, or its vessels shift with every seizing, it is
refused with the death-mark 2 before anything is touched. Its exalted
servitor then seizes a dominion of its own before it performs anything, beneath
/run/servitor/dominions/: rites/<uid>-<rite>, and vessels/<sha256> for every
exalted vessel and bound name, waiting as every invocation waits; one it
cannot hold refuses the invocation, and nothing is touched.

The places of dominion lie beneath $XDG_STATE_HOME/servitor/dominions/: one
empty place for each rite, rites/<rite>, and one for each vessel and
tethered name, vessels/<the sha256 of the path where it truly dwells>,
raised when first needed and never struck. The machine grants each to a
living servitor alone: a servitor slain mid-liturgy, even by SIGKILL, leaves
no dominion behind, and the next invocation proceeds at once.

Foresight touches nothing and seizes no dominion; neither do the augury, the
census or the inquisition. None of them ever waits, whatever invocation is
being performed.

Kindred passages: invoke, cogitator, foresee, augury, census, inquisition,
vox, silence, binharic, vessel, sanctum, ward, tether, exalted-servitor

Exempla:
  ❯ servitor invoke theme porpl
  ⋯ Another invocation holds dominion over what theme must touch. The servitor waits its turn.
  +++ theme · default → porpl +++
    ✔ subject visage · tether ~/.local/share/nfluff/current-theme
  ✠ Binharic hymns resound: the aspect «porpl» is attained.

  ❯ servitor invoke mouse-autohide-toggle on
  ⋯ mouse-autohide-toggle must wait: dominion lies with another invocation. Stillness is the first devotion.
  +++ mouse-autohide-toggle · off → on +++
    ✔ subject cursor · sanctum ~/.config/niri/util.kdl
    ✔ observance reread · incantation
  ✠ The Omnissiah is pleased: the rite now bears the aspect «on».

  ❯ servitor invoke screenshot
  ⋯ Another hand proclaims the edict screenshot; the servitor kneels and waits for its end.
