How an invocation holds dominion over its rite, and how another awaits it

Every invocation holds dominion over the rite it performs, from before its
pre-flight to its verdict: the ritual invoke as the cogitator, a rite as an
edict. It seizes the dominion before it reads the former aspect and passes
the pre-flight, and relinquishes it only once the liturgy has triumphed or
its reversion is done and the verdict is kept. Meanwhile no other invocation
of that rite performs: two invocations of one rite never interleave their
liturgies, and the later is performed after the earlier, so that the aspect
it commands is the one that remains. Rites of other names do not wait upon
one another; their invocations are performed side by side.

An invocation that finds the dominion held does not refuse. It tells once
that it waits, then waits for the dominion to be relinquished; no patience
bounds the waiting, for the holder is bounded by the patience of its own
steps. Upon a terminal the awaiting is one line before the header (upon
stderr under --binharic, withheld by --silence); without a terminal it is
the invocation's one desktop missive, as the vox commands, replaced in place
by the vox-casts that follow. Dominion free at once is never spoken of. An
interrupt (Ctrl-C) or SIGTERM ends the waiting, and nothing is touched.

The places of dominion lie beneath $XDG_STATE_HOME/servitor/dominions/: one
empty place for each rite, rites/<rite>, raised when first needed and never
struck. The machine grants each to a living servitor alone: a servitor slain
mid-liturgy, even by SIGKILL, leaves no dominion behind, and the next
invocation proceeds at once.

Foresight touches nothing and seizes no dominion; neither do the augury, the
census or the inquisition. None of them ever waits, whatever invocation is
being performed.

Kindred passages: invoke, cogitator, foresee, augury, census, inquisition,
vox, silence, binharic

Exempla:
  ❯ servitor invoke theme porpl
  ⋯ Another invocation holds dominion over what theme must touch. The servitor waits its turn.
  +++ theme · default → porpl +++
    ✔ verse 1 · tether ~/.local/share/nfluff/current-theme
  ✠ Binharic hymns resound: the aspect «porpl» is attained.

  ❯ servitor invoke screenshot
  ⋯ Another hand proclaims the edict screenshot; the servitor kneels and waits for its end.
