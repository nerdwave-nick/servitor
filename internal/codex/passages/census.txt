Take a census of every rite of the Librarium and how each stands

The census reads every rite kept in the Librarium and names, for each, the
aspect it stands in, its standing — performed, dormant, corrupted, tainted,
desecrated or heretical, and for an edict proclaimed — its aspects, its last
rite with verdict and hour, and its purpose. A rite that stands corrupted or
tainted and was also desecrated is named with both. The census reads the
subjects of every rite and awakens the auspex of each machine-spirit —
never one marked exalted — but sets nothing. The auspexes are awakened
together, never more than sixteen of their words at
once — every trial of a machine-spirit counts as words of its own — so that
slow ones labour side by side rather than one after another; the census is
told by the names of the rites all the same. With the rune --spare-auspex
it awakens none, and answers at once from the other omens, the omens the
data-slates recorded and the data-slates themselves. With the rune --vigil it tells every rite at once, line by
line in binharic, and tells again each rite whose auspex was awaited as
soon as it settles.

Roused as root by sudo, the census reads the Librarium, the vessels and the
data-slates of the one who spoke sudo, never root's, and writes nothing; the
passage on the Librarium tells all.

Spoken plainly, the census is a table with the columns RITE, ASPECT, STANDING,
ASPECTS, LAST RITE and PURPOSE; a "·" marks what is not known. A rite holding
exalted subjects, observances or verses bears beside its name the glyph of its
standing towards its sanction: ✠ sanctioned, ☓ lapsed, ○ unsanctioned. An
edict bears a "·" for its aspect and its aspects, for it stands in none. An
aspect that rests on a stale omen is marked "~", as "~on". A Librarium without
rites is told so, with the counsel to consecrate one.

With --binharic, the census is a list for other machines, and every rite bears
the keys rite, edict, aspect, standing, sanction, auspex, aspects, purpose,
desecrated, stale, last_rite, recorded_in — naming the scripture the rite was
read from — and canon, every inscription the rite declares with its purpose,
whether it is mandatory and its decrees, as the augury speaks it ([] for a
heretical rite). "auspex" tells what the auspexes of its machine-spirits gave:
"" when none keeps one the census may speak (and for an edict or a heretical
rite), "heeded", "mute", or "forgone" under --spare-auspex; the table never
shows it. "edict" is true for an edict, whose aspects are an empty list, and
false for every other rite. "sanction" tells how the rite stands towards the
sanction of what it exalts — "sanctioned", "lapsed", "unsanctioned", or ""
when it exalts nothing. "stale" is true when the rite's aspect or standing
rests on any omen taken from the data-slate's record of its last invocation,
where the live omen was not heard, and false otherwise; every rite bears it. A
rite whose data-slate cannot be read is still counted; the lament is spoken
apart from the census, and so is the lament of every forsaken sanction, whose
rite is gone or holds nothing exalted. Scripture of pattern Mark I
is read converted (see conversion).

Kindred passages: augury, standing, binharic, auspex, spare-auspex, vigil,
herald, canon, librarium, inquisition, edict, sanction

Exempla:
  servitor census
  servitor census --binharic |
    jq -r '.[] | select(.standing != "performed") | .rite'
  servitor --librarium ~/dotfiles/servitor census
  sudo servitor census
  servitor census --binharic --spare-auspex |
    jq -r '.[] | select(.auspex == "forgone") | .rite'
  servitor census --vigil | jq -c 'select(.herald == "rite")'
