Take a census of every rite of the Librarium and how each stands

The census reads every rite kept in the Librarium and names, for each, the
aspect it stands in, its standing — performed, dormant, corrupted, tainted,
desecrated or heretical, and for an edict proclaimed — its aspects, its last
rite with verdict and hour, and its purpose. A rite that stands corrupted or
tainted and was also desecrated is named with both. The census reads the
omens of every rite and awakens each auspex, but performs nothing. The
auspexes are awakened together, never more than sixteen of their words at
once — every trial of a machine-spirit counts as words of its own — so that
slow ones labour side by side rather than one after another; the census is
told by the names of the rites all the same. With the rune --spare-auspex
it awakens none, and answers at once from the other omens and the
data-slates. With the rune --vigil it tells every rite at once, line by
line in binharic, and tells again each rite whose auspex was awaited as
soon as it settles.

Roused as root by sudo, the census reads the Librarium, the vessels and the
data-slates of the one who spoke sudo, never root's, and writes nothing; the
passage on the Librarium tells all.

Spoken plainly, the census is a table with the columns RITE, ASPECT,
STANDING, ASPECTS, LAST RITE and PURPOSE; a "·" marks what is not known. A
rite holding exalted verses bears beside its name the glyph of its standing
towards its sanction: ✠ sanctioned, ☓ lapsed, ○ unsanctioned. An
edict bears a "·" for its aspect and its aspects, for it stands in none. A
Librarium without rites is told so, with the counsel to consecrate one.

With --binharic, the census is a list for other machines, and every rite
bears the keys rite, edict, aspect, standing, sanction, auspex, aspects,
purpose, desecrated, last_rite, recorded_in — naming the scripture the rite was read
from — and canon, every inscription the rite declares with its purpose,
whether it is mandatory and its decrees, as the augury speaks it ([] for a
heretical rite). "auspex" tells what the rite's auspex gave: "" when it
keeps none (and for an edict or a heretical rite), "heeded", "mute", or
"forgone" under --spare-auspex; the table never shows it. "edict" is true for an edict, whose aspects are an empty list, and
false for every other rite. "sanction" tells how the rite stands towards the sanction of its exalted
verses — "sanctioned", "lapsed", "unsanctioned", or "" when it holds none.
"stale" is true when the rite's aspect or standing rests on any omen taken
from the data-slate's record of its last invocation, where the live omen
was not heard, and false otherwise; every rite bears it. The table marks
such an aspect with "~", as "~on". A rite whose data-slate cannot be read
is still counted; the lament is
spoken apart from the census, and so is the lament of every forsaken
sanction, whose rite is gone or holds no exalted verse.

Kindred passages: augury, standing, binharic, auspex, spare-auspex, vigil,
herald, canon, librarium, inquisition, edict, sanction

Exempla:
  servitor census
  servitor census --binharic |
    jq -r '.[] | select(.standing != "performed") | .rite'
  servitor --librarium ~/dotfiles/servitor census
  sudo servitor census
  servitor census --binharic --spare-auspex |
    jq -r '.[] | select(.auspex == "forgone") | .rite'
  servitor census --vigil | jq -c 'select(.herald == "rite")'
