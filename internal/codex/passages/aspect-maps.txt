A value written once for every aspect, or once for each aspect by name

Many words of a rite must change with the aspect it is invoked into: the
scripture a sanctum keeps, the anchor of a tether, the command an
incantation speaks. Such a value may be written in either of two forms, and
the codex calls both an aspect map.

Written as a single value, it serves every aspect alike. Written as an
object, each key names a declared aspect and holds that aspect's value, and
the key "*" serves every aspect not named. An aspect named in the map is
always answered by its own value; "*" speaks only for the rest.

These values may be written as aspect maps: the scripture of a sanctum or a
transcription, the anchor of a tether, the words of an incantation, the
scroll of a litany and each of its offerings, every reversion, and the
decrees of an inscription. All else holds one value for every aspect — the
vessel and the bound name, ward, glyphs, consecrate, seal, zeal, tongue and
patience — for omens and markers must know one fixed place.

A key that names no declared aspect is heresy, for a misspelt aspect would
otherwise be served in silence by nothing. An aspect map that gives an
aspect no value and holds no "*" is heresy too: were the rite invoked into
that aspect, the servitor would not know what to do. Only decrees are
spared this, for an inscription may leave an aspect without decree.

An aspect map may still speak placeholders in each of its values, so a
single "*" with {{aspect}} often serves where many keys would be written.

Kindred passages: aspects, placeholders, decrees, scripture, anchor

Exempla:
  // one value, every aspect
  {"incantation": "niri msg action load-config-file"}

  // one value per aspect
  {"sanctum": "~/.config/niri/util.kdl",
   "scripture": {"on": "cursor {\n    hide-after-inactive-ms 400\n}",
                 "off": ""}}

  // a named aspect, and "*" for every other
  {"incantation": {"porpl": "swww img ~/walls/porpl.png",
                   "*": "swww img ~/walls/{{aspect}}.jpg"}}
