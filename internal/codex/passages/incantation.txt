An observance or edict verse that speaks one line of words in a tongue

An incantation is the plainest of the servitor's prayers: one line of
words, spoken in a tongue exactly as a supplicant would speak it at a
cogitator's prompt. It is the designation of an observance, or of a verse of
an edict's liturgy, that holds its line under "words": {"designation":
"incantation", "words": "…"}. The words are written once, never as an aspect
map, and may speak placeholders such as {{aspect}}. They are handed whole to
the tongue — the servitor awakens "<tongue> -c <words>" — so every pipe,
conjunction and quotation of the tongue is yours to command. (The enact words
of a machine-spirit are spoken alike, yet they are its own key, keyed by
omen.)

The words are spoken in the hall where the rite's scripture is kept, with
nothing offered upon their input. Their environment is the servitor's own,
anointed with SERVITOR_ASPECT (the aspect invoked), SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT
(the aspect before it, empty when unknown), SERVITOR_RITE and one
SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY> for every inscription the rite declares (the key
in capitals, every "-" and "." made "_"). An edict's words hear neither
SERVITOR_ASPECT nor SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT, not even those of an enclosing
invocation; SERVITOR_RITE and the inscriptions alone. Prefer these names to
{{inscription.<key>}} within the words: a placeholder is pasted into the
words before the tongue hears them, so a quotation mark, a "$" or a ";" in an
inscription would be read by the tongue as its own command, while a name of
the environment is read as a value and nothing more.

An incantation that ends with the death-mark 0 triumphs; any other death-mark
makes it fall, and the invocation is reverted. A failure you mean to tolerate
is written into the words themselves ("cmd || true"); the servitor knows no
other leave. It labours under its patience (30s unless the settings or the
incantation say otherwise); when it runs out, the servitor slays it together
with every process it summoned, and it falls.

What the words utter is captured, never printed: the lament of a fallen
incantation shows its last words. Children sent into the background by a
triumphant incantation live on, but the servitor listens to them only a
moment longer — send their words elsewhere ("cmd >/dev/null 2>&1 &"), lest
one that keeps speaking into the silenced channel be struck down.
Interactive words are unsupported: no terminal is lent to them, neither in
the cogitator nor when the servitor is roused from a hotkey, and their input
is empty.

An incantation yields no omen. Should the invocation fall, its "reversion" is
spoken in the same tongue to undo what it did.

An exalted incantation is spoken by the exalted servitor in its turn among
the common ones, with the machine's own authority, as its master sanctioned
it, never as the scripture stands now: "<tongue> -c <words>", its tongue
bidden to read no scripture of its own (bash, zsh, fish, sh or dash alone;
see tongue), in a working hall of its own holding its relics, never in the
rite's hall. Its environment is no longer the servitor's: it hears
PATH=/usr/local/sbin:/usr/local/bin:/usr/bin, HOME=/root, the locale, and
the SERVITOR_ variables above, nothing more. Children it sends into the
background are slain when the invocation ends; a root process meant to last
belongs in a unit of the machine ("systemd-run --unit=…").

Kindred passages: words, tongue, patience, reversion, litany, observances,
liturgy, placeholders, inscriptions, exalted, exalted-servitor

Exempla:
  {"name": "veil", "designation": "incantation",
   "words": "niri msg action do-screen-transition --delay-ms 200 || true"}

  // the inscription is read from the environment, never pasted into the words
  {"name": "remnant", "designation": "incantation", "patience": "5s",
   "words": "echo \"$SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_REASON\" >> ~/.cache/remnants"}

  {"designation": "incantation",
   "words": "swaybg -i ~/walls/visage.png >/dev/null 2>&1 &"}

  // with the machine's authority; the lasting process dwells in a unit
  {"name": "fans", "designation": "incantation", "exalted": true,
   "words": "systemd-run --unit=servitor-fan-curve fancontrol",
   "reversion": "systemctl stop servitor-fan-curve"}
