A step that speaks one line of words in the rite's tongue

An incantation is the plainest of the servitor's prayers: one line of words,
spoken in a tongue exactly as a supplicant would speak it at a cogitator's
prompt. The step's own key holds those words, and they may differ for every
aspect as an aspect map, or serve all aspects through placeholders such as
{{aspect}}. The words are handed whole to the tongue — the servitor awakens
"<tongue> -c <words>" — so every pipe, conjunction and quotation of the
tongue is yours to command.

The words are spoken in the hall where the rite's scripture is kept, with
nothing offered upon their input. Their environment is the servitor's own,
anointed with SERVITOR_ASPECT (the aspect invoked), SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT
(the aspect before it, empty when unknown), SERVITOR_RITE and one
SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY> for every inscription the rite declares (the key
in capitals, every "-" and "." made "_"). Prefer these names to
{{inscription.<key>}} within the words: a placeholder is pasted into the
words before the tongue hears them, so a quotation mark, a "$" or a ";" in an
inscription would be read by the tongue as its own command, while a name of
the environment is read as a value and nothing more.

An incantation that ends with the death-mark 0 triumphs; any other death-mark
makes it fall, and the invocation is reverted. A failure you mean to tolerate
is written into the words themselves ("cmd || true"); the servitor knows no
other leave. The step labours under its patience (30s unless the settings or
the step say otherwise); when it runs out, the servitor slays the step
together with every process it summoned, and the step falls.

What the words utter is captured, never printed: the lament of a fallen step
shows its last words. Children sent into the background by a triumphant
incantation live on, but the servitor listens to them only a moment longer —
send their words elsewhere ("cmd >/dev/null 2>&1 &"), lest one that keeps
speaking into the silenced channel be struck down. Interactive words are
unsupported: no terminal is lent to them, neither in the cogitator nor when
the servitor is roused from a hotkey, and their input is empty.

An incantation yields no omen. Should the invocation fall, its "reversion" is
spoken in the same tongue to undo what it did.

Kindred passages: tongue, patience, reversion, litany, placeholders,
inscriptions

Exempla:
  {"incantation": "niri msg action do-screen-transition"}

  {"incantation": {"on": "makoctl mode -a do-not-disturb",
                   "off": "makoctl mode -r do-not-disturb"},
   "reversion": {"on": "makoctl mode -r do-not-disturb",
                 "off": "makoctl mode -a do-not-disturb"}}

  // the inscription is read from the environment, never pasted into the words
  {"incantation": "echo \"$SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_REASON\" >> ~/.cache/remnants",
   "patience": "5s"}

  {"incantation": "swaybg -i ~/walls/{{aspect}}.png >/dev/null 2>&1 &"}
