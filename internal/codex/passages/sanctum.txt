A subject that keeps a warded region of a vessel and rewrites it alone

The sanctum is the most reverent of the designations. A subject designated
"sanctum" names under "vessel" a scripture-file of the machine, and within
that vessel it keeps a warded region between two markers. Each invocation
that changes it rewrites the scripture between the markers and nothing
else; every line outside them is inviolate, so the servitor may share a
vessel with the hands that wrote the rest of it.

The markers are written in the vessel's own glyphs, so the vessel's own
machine-spirit reads them as mere remarks:

  <glyph> +++ begin of sanctum <ward> -- aspect|<aspect> +++ <closing-glyph>
  <glyph> +++ end of sanctum <ward> +++ <closing-glyph>

The begin marker records the aspect the sanctum was last invoked into; of
the key|value pairs after "--" only aspect| is read and all others are
passed over. When the vessel holds no sanctum of this ward yet, the sanctum
is appended to the vessel's end after a blank line; when it holds one, the
sanctum is rewritten in its place and keeps its indent.

Its "omens" are the scripture it holds for each aspect — inline words, a
list of lines or a tome, keyed by aspect with "*" for every other. Empty
scripture ("") keeps an empty sanctum, markers and all. Null scripture is
heresy: a sanctum is never struck from its vessel, for its marker is what
remembers the aspect. Scripture that would carry one of the sanctum's own
markers into the vessel is heresy too.

"ward" names the sanctum within its vessel and defaults to the rite's name.
"glyph" and "closing-glyph" are divined from the vessel's extension unless
written. "consecrate": true brings a missing vessel into being (sealed 0644);
without it a missing vessel is heresy in the pre-flight. Halls are never
raised: the hall that is to hold the vessel must already stand. A sanctum
reads and sets itself, and bears no auspex, enact words, tongue or patience.

The vessel's path may speak placeholders; a leading "~" and the $NAMES of
the environment are expanded, and a path not rooted at "/" is reckoned from
the rites/ hall of the Librarium. A vessel that is a tether is followed: the
scripture-file at the far end is rewritten, the tether itself remains. Every
write is whole and sudden — a fresh scripture-file takes the old one's place
at once — and keeps the seal the vessel bore.

The sanctum yields an omen: the aspect its begin marker records. At
invocation it is unchanged when its marker names the aspect invoked and its
region holds that aspect's scripture, untainted; a region that holds the
scripture but whose marker names another aspect is changing, so that its
marker is rewritten. Should hands other than the servitor's alter the
scripture between the markers, so that it is no longer the scripture of the
recorded aspect, the sanctum is tainted; the augury names its subject under
"taint" and the inquisition notes it as impurity, and the next invocation
writes it anew. Markers begun twice, never sealed, or sealed without
beginning are heresy.

Should the invocation fall, the reversion returns the vessel to the very
scripture it held before, and strikes a vessel the sanctum consecrated.

Exalted ("exalted": true), the sanctum is rewritten by the exalted servitor
from the sanction of its master, never by the servitor: its vessel must
begin at the root, beneath none of /proc, /sys and /dev, and is walked
through halls the root alone may write and written whole, keeping its owner
and seal; one it consecrates belongs to the root. Should its invoker be
forbidden to read the vessel, the exalted servitor decides at invocation
whether it changes, and answers only that, never its scripture; foresight
tells its owner, seal and size, "contents hidden — change unknown". The
exalted servitor reverts it should the invocation fall.

An edict may keep no sanctum: a sanctum's marker records an aspect, and an
edict bears none. In the elder pattern "Mark I" the sanctum was a step of
the liturgy, its own key holding the vessel and "scripture" its scripture;
conversion makes it a subject named "sanctum-<verse>" whose omens are that
scripture, and leaves its markers as they stand.

Kindred passages: ward, glyph, closing-glyph, consecrate, scripture, vessel,
omens, standing, subjects, designation, edict, exalted
