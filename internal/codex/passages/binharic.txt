Speak in binharic, the tongue of machines, for other machines to read

The rune --binharic is given to the census, to the Inquisition and to the
ritual invoke. Their answers are then rendered not as tables and sentences
for the faithful but as binharic for lesser machines: objects with stable
keys, which other commands may parse — jq foremost among them.

The census in binharic is a list in which every rite bears rite, edict,
aspect, standing, aspects, purpose, desecrated, last_rite and recorded_in;
an edict bears "edict": true and an empty list of aspects. The
Inquisition in binharic is a list of findings, each bearing scripture, line,
column, rite, judgement ("heresy" or "impurity") and denunciation; its exit
remains 1 when any heresy was found.

An invocation in binharic speaks one line upon stdout: the very line it
added to the chronicle — at, rite, edict, aspect, former, inscriptions,
verdict, fell_at, heresy and reversions, an edict bearing no aspect and no
former — drawn from the same record, so the two never disagree. Should the
pre-flight forbid it, it speaks instead the rite, the aspect and every heresy
under "forbidden". Its report and laments go to stderr, so stdout bears the
binharic alone, at a terminal or without one; its death-mark tells the
verdict all the same. Foresight knows no binharic.

The augury needs no such rune: spoken without a key it always answers in
binharic, and spoken with a key it answers with that single value.

Kindred passages: census, inquisition, augury, invoke, forbidden,
death-marks

Exempla:
  servitor census --binharic | jq -r '.[] | "\(.rite) \(.aspect)"'
  servitor inquisition --binharic | jq length
  servitor invoke theme porpl --binharic | jq -r .verdict
