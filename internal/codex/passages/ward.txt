The name that marks one sanctum within its vessel

A ward is the name a sanctum bears inside its markers, the sigil by which the
servitor finds its own region amid the rest of a vessel's scripture. It is
written into both markers:

  # +++ begin of sanctum <ward> -- aspect|on +++
  # +++ end of sanctum <ward> +++

When a sanctum writes no "ward", the rite's own name serves as its ward.
Wards exist so that many sanctums — of one rite or of several — may dwell in
the same vessel side by side, each rewriting only its own region.

A ward may not be empty and may hold no whitespace, for it is written into
the markers. Nor may it hold the sanctum's closing glyph, which would end
the marker in its midst. The ward is never illuminated: placeholders have no
power there, for the marker must be found again by every augury.

One ward may be kept only once in one vessel. Should two sanctums — in one
rite or across the rites of the Librarium — claim the same ward in the same
vessel, the inquisition denounces it as heresy, for they would overwrite each
other's scripture. Vessels are compared after "~", $NAMES and the rite's hall
are reckoned, so two spellings of one path do not escape judgement. A vessel
found to hold the same ward begun twice is heresy as well.

Changing a ward abandons the old sanctum: its markers remain in the vessel,
and a new sanctum is appended under the new ward at the next invocation.

Kindred passages: sanctum, glyph, closing-glyph, vessel, inquisition

Exempla:
  {"sanctum": "~/.config/niri/util.kdl", "ward": "cursor",
   "scripture": {"on": "cursor { hide-after-inactive-ms 400; }", "off": ""}}

  {"sanctum": "~/.config/niri/util.kdl", "ward": "gaps",
   "scripture": {"*": "layout { gaps 8; }"}}
