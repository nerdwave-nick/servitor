A subject that writes a whole vessel anew, or strikes it, for each aspect

Where a sanctum keeps one warded region, a transcription claims the entire
vessel. A subject designated "transcription" names its "vessel"; its
"omens" are what the whole vessel shall hold in each aspect — inline words,
a list of lines or a tome, keyed by aspect with "*" for every other. An omen
written null decrees that the vessel shall not be at all: invoking that
aspect strikes the vessel from the machine, and a vessel already absent is
left absent.

Because the transcription overwrites everything, it guards the work of other
hands. A vessel that already stands is replaced (or struck) only when its
scripture is the omen of one of the rite's aspects — that is, when the
servitor recognises it as its own work, one final newline forgiven. Any
other vessel halts the pre-flight, for the servitor keeps no copy of what it
casts down, unless the subject burns with "zeal": true. A hall standing
where the vessel should be never gives way.

A vessel the transcription brings into being bears the seal 0644 unless
"seal" decrees another; a vessel that stands keeps its seal, or takes the
"seal" written. The hall that is to hold the vessel must already stand; the
servitor never raises halls. The vessel's path may speak placeholders; "~"
and $NAMES are expanded, and a path not rooted at "/" is reckoned from the
rites/ hall of the Librarium. A vessel that is a tether is followed, and the
scripture-file at its far end is transcribed. Every write is whole and
sudden, so no reader ever beholds half a vessel. A transcription reads and
sets itself, and bears no auspex, enact words, tongue or patience.

The transcription yields an omen: every aspect whose rendered scripture the
vessel holds now, or every aspect whose omen is null when the vessel is
absent. A vessel that matches no aspect yields no omen. At invocation it is
unchanged when it already holds the omen of the aspect invoked, and passed
over.

Should the invocation fall, the reversion restores the vessel exactly as it
was found — its former scripture and seal — or strikes it again if it did
not stand before.

Exalted ("exalted": true), the transcription is performed by the exalted
servitor from the sanction of its master, never by the servitor: its vessel
must begin at the root, beneath none of /proc, /sys and /dev, and is walked
through halls the root alone may write; a vessel of one name only, bearing
no extended attributes, is written whole, keeping its owner, and its seal
unless "seal" decrees one; a vessel it brings into being belongs to the
root. A standing vessel gives way only to the scripture of an aspect of the
sanction, or to zeal. Should its invoker be forbidden to read the vessel,
the exalted servitor decides at invocation whether it changes, and answers
only that; foresight tells its owner, seal and size and the SHA-256 of the
scripture it shall hold, "contents hidden — change unknown". The exalted
servitor reverts it should the invocation fall, and tells of it only sizes
and SHA-256 sums, never its scripture; nor is its scripture ever recorded
on the data-slate.

An edict may perform no transcription: a transcribed vessel bears the
scripture of one aspect and reveals that aspect thereafter, and an edict
bears none. In the elder pattern "Mark I" the transcription was a step of
the liturgy, its own key holding the vessel and "scripture" what it should
hold; conversion makes it a subject named "transcription-<verse>" whose
omens are that scripture.

Kindred passages: scripture, seal, zeal, tome, illuminate, vessel, omens,
subjects, designation, edict, exalted
