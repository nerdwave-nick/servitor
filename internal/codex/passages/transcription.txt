A step that writes a whole vessel anew, or strikes it, for each aspect

Where a sanctum keeps one warded region, a transcription claims the entire
vessel. Its own key names the vessel; its "scripture" is what the whole
vessel shall hold in each aspect, as an aspect map of inline words, a list
of lines or a tome. Written null for an aspect, the scripture decrees that
the vessel shall not be at all: invoking that aspect strikes the vessel from
the machine, and a vessel already absent is left absent.

Because the transcription overwrites everything, it guards the work of other
hands. A vessel that already stands is replaced (or struck) only when its
scripture is the scripture of one of the rite's aspects — that is, when the
servitor recognises it as its own work, one final newline forgiven. Any
other vessel halts the pre-flight, for the servitor keeps no copy of what it
casts down, unless the step burns with "zeal": true. A hall standing where
the vessel should be never gives way.

A vessel the transcription brings into being bears the seal 0644 unless
"seal" decrees another; a vessel that stands keeps its seal, or takes the
"seal" written. The hall that is to hold the vessel must already stand; the
servitor never raises halls. The vessel's path may speak placeholders; "~"
and $NAMES are expanded, and a path not rooted at "/" is reckoned from the
rites/ hall of the Librarium. A vessel that is a tether is followed, and the
scripture-file at its far end is transcribed. Every write is whole and
sudden, so no reader ever beholds half a vessel.

The transcription yields an omen: the aspect whose rendered scripture the
vessel holds now, or the aspect of null scripture when the vessel is absent.
A vessel that matches no aspect yields no omen.

Should the invocation fall, the reversion restores the vessel exactly as it
was found — its former scripture and seal — or strikes it again if it did
not stand before.

Exalted ("exalted": true), the transcription is performed by the exalted
servitor from the sanction of its master, never by the servitor: its vessel
must begin at the root, beneath none of /proc, /sys and /dev, and is walked
through halls the root alone may write; a vessel of one name only, bearing
no extended attributes, is written whole, keeping its owner, and its seal
unless "seal" decrees one; a vessel it brings into being belongs to the
root. A standing vessel gives way only to the scripture of an aspect of the
sanction, or to zeal. Its invoker may be forbidden to read the vessel: the
pre-flight then plans it with its change unknown, and foresight tells its
owner, seal and size and the SHA-256 of the scripture it shall hold,
"contents hidden — change unknown". The exalted servitor reverts it should
the invocation fall, and tells of it only sizes and SHA-256 sums, never its
scripture.

An edict may perform no transcription: a transcribed vessel bears the
scripture of one aspect and reveals that aspect thereafter, and an edict
bears none. A transcription in an edict's liturgy is heresy.

Kindred passages: scripture, seal, zeal, tome, illuminate, vessel, omens, edict,
exalted
