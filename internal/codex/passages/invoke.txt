Invoke an aspect of a rite, or proclaim an edict

Invocation is the servitor's foremost ritual. Name a rite and one of its
aspects, and the servitor brings the machine into that aspect: it reads
every subject of the rite, sets only those that differ, and proves each one
it set. Every rite of the Librarium stands beneath this ritual as a ritual
of its own, so the faithful write: servitor invoke <rite> <aspect>.

An edict knows no aspect and is proclaimed by its name alone: servitor
invoke <edict>. Any word written after its name is refused before a verse
is performed, for an edict bears no aspect. Every invocation of an edict
performs its whole liturgy anew, verse by verse; nothing is passed over
because it was performed before.

First comes the pre-flight, which touches nothing: every omen, enact word,
observance, verse and tome is rendered for the aspect invoked, every vessel,
tether and tongue weighed, and every inscription resolved. Should the
pre-flight find any heresy, the invocation is forbidden, each heresy is
recited, and nothing on the machine is disturbed.

Then every subject is read, as the augury reads it, and the former aspect
is the one their omens agree upon — the one aspect they fit, or the
data-slate's among several — else it is unknown. A subject already bearing
the omen of the aspect invoked is unchanged and passed over; every other —
one whose omen differs, whose auspex is mute, or that has no auspex — is
changing. When nothing changes, nothing is performed, and the invocation is
still a triumph: the data-slate is written, the chronicle kept, and a
"success" vox-cast still speaks.

The changing subjects are set in written order. Before each, its introits
not yet spoken; then the subject is set — a sanctum, transcription or tether
written as its designation commands, a machine-spirit by its enact words for
the omen commanded — and proven at once by a reading afresh, which must
show that omen; a machine-spirit without an auspex cannot be proven. After
it, each recessional of which it is the last changing subject to name it.
Every observance is spoken once, in place, and only when its guards "to" and
"from" admit the invocation.

Should anything fall — a vessel that cannot be written, enact words or an
observance that end bearing a death-mark, a patience that runs out, a proof
that shows another omen — everything already performed is reverted in
reverse order, the fallen one among them: each subject restored to what was
read, each observance undone by its reversion words, as the passage on
reversion tells. The lament tells what fell — for a subject, the omen
commanded and the omen found — its last words, and how each reversion went.
An interrupt from the terminal (Ctrl-C) halts the invocation in the same
manner: the labouring words and their kin are slain, nothing further
begins, and every deed is undone; a second interrupt during the reversions
is not heeded, so that the reversions may finish. SIGTERM halts it alike.
A hangup does not: the departure of the one who invoked it is no command to
undo, and a reader gone from stdout or stderr loses only the words, never a
deed. Lesser machines that do not wait launch the servitor detached, as its
own passage teaches.

A rite whose subjects, observances or verses are exalted is performed alike,
yet its exalted ones never with its invoker's authority. Its pre-flight
also draws them for the aspect invoked into their unit and compares its
sigil with the sanction of their master: a plan never sanctioned, or whose
sanction has lapsed, is refused before any proof is asked — even when
nothing exalted would change — and the words name servitor sanction <rite>.
It judges the servitor installed at /usr/bin/servitor and finds the
intercessor; it refuses a rune given for an inscription spliced into
exalted words, and a word given at invocation that exalted speech could not
hear (more than 4 KiB, or bearing a NUL or a newline). The exalted servitor
is raised only when an exalted subject or observance is to change, or when
an auspex itself marked exalted must be read: so an invocation that changes
nothing exalted asks no proof at all. Then one line tells, upon a terminal,
that the exalted words await the master's proof, naming the intercessor
and the sigil (upon stderr under --binharic, withheld by --silence; without
a terminal the intercessor's own dialog speaks), and the intercessor asks
the proof once. The one exalted servitor it raises first reads every
subject only the root may read, then sets each exalted subject and speaks
each exalted observance in its turn, from the sanctioned copy alone, while
the servitor performs the common ones between them; should anything fall,
the exalted servitor restores its own among the common reversions, and no
second proof is asked. A proof dismissed or refused is a refusal before
anything was touched. Every invocation asks the proof anew.

Each inscription the rite declares becomes a rune of its own, --<key> <value>,
which outranks the rite's decree for that aspect (or the edict's one decree);
an empty word clears it. A mandatory inscription that neither rune nor decree
supplies forbids the invocation. The rune --foresee (-f) reveals everything
the invocation would do and performs none of it; --silence (-s) withholds the
report, save the vox-casts the rite asks for. The rune --binharic speaks
upon stdout, for lesser machines, the very line the invocation added to the
chronicle — or, should the pre-flight forbid it, the heresies under
"forbidden" — and tells the report upon stderr instead; the desktop hears
as it always would. --binharic and --foresee are not spoken together.

Spoken at a terminal, the invocation is told in one report as it is
performed: a header naming the rite and its turning, the former aspect and
the new; a line for every subject set and every observance spoken once it
is performed, the home written ~ and a long line cut short with … to the
width of the terminal; every progress vox-cast in its place, with how many
of the changing subjects are done; and one closing line of triumph, drawn
from the rite's success vox-cast or, should it have none, from the same
litany. An invocation that changes nothing tells its header and its closing
line alone:

  ❯ servitor invoke theme porpl
  +++ theme · default → porpl +++
    ✔ observance veil · incantation
    ✔ subject visage · tether ~/.local/share/nfluff/current-theme
    ⋯ Cogs turn: visage now bears the aspect «porpl».  (1/1)
    ✔ observance awaken · litany
  ✠ Every rune turned, every litany sung: the aspect «porpl» prevails.

  ❯ servitor invoke theme porpl
  +++ theme · porpl → porpl +++
  ✠ The tech-priests rejoice; the machine wears the aspect «porpl».

Exalted subjects await the master's proof before the header, the proof
asked in the intercessor's own words:

  ❯ servitor invoke split-lock on
  ⋯ The exalted deeds of split-lock await the master's proof: sudo asks it, under the sigil 4f0c2a9e1b7d.
  servitor: authenticate to perform split-lock (sigil 4f0c2a9e1b7d):
  +++ split-lock · off → on +++
    ✔ subject penance · machine-spirit
    ✔ subject remembrance · transcription /etc/sysctl.d/90-split-lock.conf
  ✠ The Omnissiah is pleased: the rite now bears the aspect «on».

An edict's header names the edict alone, with no turning of aspects, a line
for every verse performed, and its closing line names the edict, never an
aspect:

  ❯ servitor invoke screenshot
  +++ screenshot · edict +++
    ✔ verse 1 · incantation grim -g "$(slurp)" ~/pictures/visage.png
    ✔ verse 2 · incantation wl-copy --type image/png < ~/pictures/visage.png
  ✠ The edict screenshot is proclaimed, and the machine obeys.

The closing words are drawn at random. Roused without a terminal — from a
hotkey or a launcher — the servitor sends the vox-casts to the desktop
instead, and tells the same report once the rite has triumphed. Should
anything fall, the report ends with the last deed performed, and the lament
alone tells the fall.

After a performance the servitor writes the rite's data-slate — with the
omens it knew at the end — and adds one line to the chronicle, naming the
subjects it changed, whatever the verdict. An invocation the pre-flight
forbade, or one merely foreseen, is neither chronicled nor recorded on the
data-slate, for nothing was performed. Scripture of pattern Mark I
is converted before it is invoked (see conversion).

The servitor's death-mark tells how the invocation ended, with or without
--binharic: 0 upon triumph, 1 when it was reverted, 2 when it was refused
before anything was touched (the pre-flight forbade it, the rite or aspect
is unknown, a word was written after an edict's name, a rune was
malformed, its exalted subjects and observances stand unsanctioned or
lapsed, no intercessor vouched for the master, the proof was dismissed),
and 3 when it faltered.

The servitor itself performs nothing with the machine's own authority; the
exalted servitor, raised for one invocation, performs what is exalted.
Roused as root — through sudo, run0 or pkexec, or by root itself — it
refuses every invocation and every proclaiming with the death-mark 2 before
anything is touched; only --foresee is still heeded. Rouse it as yourself.
Under sudo the foresight reads the Librarium, the vessels and the
data-slates of the one who spoke sudo, as the passage on the Librarium
tells.

A rite's scripture, kept in <Librarium>/rites/<name>.json, may read thus:

  {
    "pattern": "Mark II",
    "purpose": "Silence the heralds of the machine",
    "aspects": ["on", "off"],
    "vox-cast": ["success"],
    "subjects": [
      {"name": "mako", "designation": "machine-spirit",
       "omens": {"on": "do-not-disturb", "*": "default"},
       "auspex": {"scan": "makoctl mode"},
       "enact": {"*": "makoctl mode -s \"$SERVITOR_OMEN\""},
       "introit": ["herald-silence"]}
    ],
    "observances": [
      {"name": "herald-silence", "designation": "incantation", "to": ["on"],
       "words": "notify-send -t 2000 'The heralds fall silent'"}
    ]
  }

Kindred passages: rite, edict, subjects, observances, liturgy, pre-flight,
reversion, inscriptions, verdicts, binharic, forbidden, death-marks,
detached, dominion, exalted, sanction, intercessor

Exempla:
  servitor invoke mouse-autohide-toggle on --reason "gaming remnant"
  servitor invoke mouse-autohide-toggle off --foresee
  servitor invoke theme "$(printf 'default\nfinii\nporpl\n' | fuzzel --dmenu)"
  servitor invoke screenshot --region whole
  servitor invoke theme porpl --binharic | jq -r .verdict
