Recite a passage of the codex, the servitor's book of instruction

The codex holds the lore of the servitor: for every ritual, rune, step and
key — and for the deeper mysteries of omens, standing, verdicts and the rest
— one verbose passage, as written in the codex, each bearing at least one
example. Expounding recites it.

Spoken alone, servitor expound recites the index of the codex: its chapters
of rituals, runes, steps of the liturgy, keys of a rite's scripture, keys of
the settings and further lore, each topic with the single line that sums it
up. Spoken with a topic, it recites that one passage. A topic is named as the
faithful would write it in scripture or upon the command line, runes without
their dashes: servitor expound tether, servitor expound foresee. The passage
of a ritual is followed by its invocation and its runes, as the servitor
itself knows them, and the passage of invoke also names every rite of the
Librarium.

A topic the codex does not hold is denounced, and the topics that most
resemble it are offered in its stead. Completion offers every topic of the
index, each with its summing line.

Know the servitor in brief, as the codex teaches it: a rite, kept in the
Librarium, knows a fixed set of aspects and a liturgy of steps performed in
the order written — sanctums, transcriptions, tethers, incantations, litanies
and vox-casts. Should a step fall, every deed already done is reverted. A
sanctum rewrites only its warded region of a vessel, and appends itself to
the vessel's end when not yet kept:

  // +++ begin of sanctum cursor -- aspect|on +++
  <scripture kept by the servitor>
  // +++ end of sanctum cursor +++

Every invocation is recorded in the chronicle; the augury, the census and
the Inquisition tell how the rites stand.

Kindred passages: invoke, rite, liturgy, cogitator, completion

Exempla:
  servitor expound
  servitor expound tether
