The examination of every step before an invocation touches the machine

Before a single step is performed, the servitor examines the whole liturgy
against a shadow of the machine, changing nothing. This is the pre-flight,
and every invocation passes through it.

In the pre-flight the servitor resolves the inscriptions — runes first, then
the decrees of the aspect, or an edict's one decree — and denounces a
mandatory inscription left without value. It renders every placeholder of
every step for the aspect invoked (an edict's for none), and reads every tome
afresh. An aspect offered to an edict is refused. It checks that each vessel
may be written: that the halls above it stand, that a sanctum's vessel exists
or may be consecrated, that a transcription replaces only scripture of the
rite's own aspects unless with zeal, and that a tether rebinds only a tether.
It seeks every tongue along the machine's PATH, and the scroll of every
litany.

For a rite with exalted verses the pre-flight does more, before any proof
is asked: it draws the exalted verses for the aspect into their unit and
compares its sigil with the sanction (unsanctioned or lapsed is refused,
naming servitor sanction <rite>); it judges the servitor installed at
/usr/bin/servitor and finds the intercessor; it refuses a rune of an
inscription spliced into exalted words, a word exalted speech could not
hear, and an exalted tongue other than bash, zsh, fish, sh or dash. An
exalted vessel its invoker may not read is no heresy: it is planned with
its change unknown, for the exalted servitor reads it.

Each step is planned upon the shadow as the steps before it left it, so a
litany may recite a scroll that a transcription earlier in the same liturgy
brings into being, and a sanctum may follow a tether bound a verse before.

Should any heresy be found, the invocation is refused whole: every heresy is
spoken, nothing is performed, and neither the data-slate nor the chronicle is
touched. The servitor then bears the death-mark 2, and in binharic speaks
the heresies under "forbidden". Only when the pre-flight is pure does the liturgy begin; whatever
falls thereafter falls in the performance, and is reverted.

To foresee is to halt after the pre-flight and reveal what it planned: each
vessel's changes, each tether's rebinding, each command as it would be
spoken — without awakening even the auspex.

Kindred passages: invoke, foresee, heresy, inscriptions, placeholders

Exempla:
  servitor invoke theme porpl --foresee

  servitor ✠ the pre-flight forbids the invocation of "theme" into "porpl";
  nothing was touched:
    …

  servitor ✠ the pre-flight forbids the proclaiming of the edict
  "screenshot"; nothing was touched:
    …
