The examination of a whole invocation before it touches the machine

Before a single subject is set or a verse spoken, the servitor examines the
whole invocation against a shadow of the machine, changing nothing. This is
the pre-flight, and every invocation passes through it.

In the pre-flight the servitor resolves the inscriptions — runes first, then
the decrees of the aspect, or an edict's one decree — and denounces a
mandatory inscription left without value. It renders every placeholder for
the aspect invoked (an edict's for none): every omen, every enact word,
every observance — those its guards will pass over too — and every verse,
and reads every tome afresh. An aspect offered to an edict is refused. It
checks that each vessel may be written: that the halls above it stand, that
a sanctum's vessel exists or may be consecrated, that a transcription
replaces only scripture of the rite's own aspects unless with zeal, and that
a tether rebinds only a tether. It seeks every tongue along the machine's
PATH, and the scroll of every litany.

For a rite with exalted subjects, observances or verses the pre-flight does
more, before any proof is asked and before any subject is read: it draws
them for the aspect into their unit and compares its sigil with the sanction
(unsanctioned or lapsed is refused, naming servitor sanction <rite>, even
when nothing exalted would change); it judges the servitor installed at
/usr/bin/servitor and finds the intercessor; it refuses a rune of an
inscription spliced into exalted words, a word exalted speech could not
hear, and an exalted tongue other than bash, zsh, fish, sh or dash. An
exalted vessel its invoker may not read is no heresy: the exalted servitor
decides it at invocation.

Each subject is planned upon the shadow as the subjects before it left it,
and the observances and verses upon the shadow the subjects leave, so a
litany may recite a scroll that a transcription of the same rite brings into
being; the scroll is sought on the disk again ere it is recited.

Should any heresy be found, the invocation is refused whole: every heresy is
spoken, nothing is read or performed, and neither the data-slate nor the
chronicle is touched. The servitor then bears the death-mark 2, and in
binharic speaks the heresies under "forbidden". Only when the pre-flight is
pure are the subjects read and set; whatever falls thereafter falls in the
performance, and is reverted.

To foresee is to halt after the pre-flight and the reading, and reveal what
was planned: each subject unchanged or changing, each vessel's changes, each
tether's rebinding, each command as it would be spoken — without speaking an
enact word, an observance, a verse, or an auspex marked exalted.

Kindred passages: invoke, foresee, heresy, inscriptions, placeholders

Exempla:
  servitor invoke theme porpl --foresee

  servitor ✠ the pre-flight forbids the invocation of "theme" into "porpl";
  nothing was touched:
    …

  servitor ✠ the pre-flight forbids the proclaiming of the edict
  "screenshot"; nothing was touched:
    …
