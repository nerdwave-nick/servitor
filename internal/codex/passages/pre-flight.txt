The examination of every step before an invocation touches the machine

Before a single step is performed, the servitor examines the whole liturgy
against a shadow of the machine, changing nothing. This is the pre-flight,
and every invocation passes through it.

In the pre-flight the servitor resolves the inscriptions — runes first, then
the decrees of the aspect — and denounces a mandatory inscription left
without value. It renders every placeholder of every step for the aspect
invoked, and reads every tome afresh. It checks that each vessel may be
written: that the halls above it stand, that a sanctum's vessel exists or may
be consecrated, that a transcription replaces only scripture of the rite's
own aspects unless with zeal, and that a tether rebinds only a tether. It
seeks every tongue along the machine's PATH, and the scroll of every litany.

Each step is planned upon the shadow as the steps before it left it, so a
litany may recite a scroll that a transcription earlier in the same liturgy
brings into being, and a sanctum may follow a tether bound a verse before.

Should any heresy be found, the invocation is refused whole: every heresy is
spoken, nothing is performed, and neither the data-slate nor the chronicle is
touched. Only when the pre-flight is pure does the liturgy begin; whatever
falls thereafter falls in the performance, and is reverted.

To foresee is to halt after the pre-flight and reveal what it planned: each
vessel's changes, each tether's rebinding, each command as it would be
spoken — without awakening even the auspex.

Kindred passages: invoke, foresee, heresy, inscriptions, placeholders

Exempla:
  servitor invoke theme porpl --foresee

  servitor ✠ the pre-flight forbids the invocation of "theme" into "porpl";
