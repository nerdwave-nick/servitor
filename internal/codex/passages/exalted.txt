A subject, observance or verse set with the machine's own authority, from a sanction

Some subjects reach beyond what their invoker may touch: the knobs of the
kernel beneath /sys, sysctl, the scriptures of /etc, the units of the
machine itself. Write "exalted": true upon such a subject, and it is set not
with its invoker's authority but with the machine's own, by an exalted
servitor raised for that one invocation — and only as its master sanctioned
it. Everything else is performed as ever, with the invoker's authority.

"exalted": true may stand upon a subject of any designation, upon an
observance, and upon a verse of an edict's liturgy. It is true or false,
never an aspect map; false is as if unwritten. It exalts what sets the
subject — the writing of a sanctum, transcription or tether, or the enact
words of a machine-spirit — and the words of an observance or verse; never
the auspex that reads a subject, which speaks with its invoker's authority
unless it is itself marked "exalted".

The words of what is exalted are fixed when its master sanctions them:
enact words, words, scrolls, reversions, vessels, bound names, omens,
offerings, and scripture inline or illuminated from a tome. {{aspect}},
{{rite.name}}, {{omen}} — every omen a machine-spirit declares is fixed at
the sanction — and an inscription taken from its decrees may be spliced into
them; {{former}} may not, nor a mandatory inscription that some aspect does
not decree, for their words are given only at invocation. Exalted speech
hears them from its environment instead: "$SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT",
"$SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY>" and, for enact words, "$SERVITOR_OMEN".

An exalted vessel or bound name must begin at the root once rendered, and
may not lie beneath /proc, /sys or /dev: the knobs of the kernel are set by
the enact words of an exalted machine-spirit, never as vessels. Scriptures
that exalted speech reads beside its scroll are named among its relics.

No exalted subject, observance or verse is performed without the sanction of
its master, laid with servitor sanction <rite> and withdrawn with servitor
abjure <rite>, and none is ever performed by the servitor that invokes it.
Invoked, the rite's pre-flight compares the sigil of the plan for the
aspect with the sanction: unsanctioned or lapsed, the invocation is refused
before any proof is asked — even when nothing exalted would change — its
death-mark 2, and the words name servitor sanction <rite>.

Sanctioned, the rite reads every subject first and plans what changes: the
exalted servitor is raised only when an exalted subject or observance is to
change, so an invocation that changes nothing exalted asks no proof of the
master. Raised, the intercessor asks the master's proof once and raises one
exalted servitor, which sets each exalted subject in its place among the
common ones and speaks each exalted observance in its place, its "to" and
"from" heeded, from the sanctioned copy alone. The invoker proves each
machine-spirit by its own auspex; should the invocation fall, the exalted
servitor restores each exalted subject — a machine-spirit to the omen it was
read to show, by the restoring words its master sanctioned — among the
common reversions, without a second proof. A proof dismissed touches nothing
(death-mark 2, no chronicle). The chronicle names the intercessor and the
sigil. An edict raises the exalted servitor at every proclaiming that holds
an exalted verse.

What only the root can read — nft list ruleset, /sys/kernel/debug, a
scroll sealed 0600 — an exalted subject reads by an auspex itself marked
"exalted": {"scan": "nft list table inet gaming", "exalted": true}. Its
words, tongue, patience and relics are part of the sanction, so editing
them lapses every aspect. A rite holding such an auspex always raises the
exalted servitor, since what changes cannot be known without it; it reads
every such subject at once, before anything is performed, and matches what
each printed against the subject's declared omens itself, telling only the
omens that fit, or none: never the words printed. So too an exalted
sanctum, transcription or tether its invoker may not read: the exalted
servitor tells only whether it already stands as the aspect demands, never
its scripture. Then the plan is whole, and what stands as demanded is
passed over. The exalted servitor proves what it set or restored of such a
subject by the same auspex, and the omens it was told to show are kept on
the data-slate, marked "exalted". The augury, the census, the vigil,
completion and foresight never speak such an auspex; they tell the omen
last recorded, stale, and foresight that it is read at invocation when none
is.

--foresee reveals what the invocation would do, marks each exalted subject,
observance and verse ✠ and tells the sanction standing, and never exalts.
The cogitator performs them alike, lending sudo its terminal while it asks,
and sanctions and abjures with s.

The inquisition notes the impurities of exaltation: sudo, run0, pkexec or
doas within words that are not exalted (write "exalted": true instead),
exalted speech reaching into the invoker's home ($HOME, ~) or for scripture
beside it that is not among its relics, a program the machine guards by an
intercessor of its own (systemctl, hostnamectl, …) called by words that are
not exalted beside exalted ones, and an exalted machine-spirit without an
auspex, set as root at every invocation, whose remedy is an auspex itself
marked exalted.

In the elder pattern "Mark I" any step but a vox-cast might be exalted.
Conversion keeps "exalted" upon the subjects, observances and verses it
makes, and the sanction of the rite lapses, for the unit sanctioned changes
with its pattern: sanction it anew (see conversion).

Kindred passages: sanction, abjure, sigil, relics, intercessor, exalted-servitor,
subjects, machine-spirit, observances, invoke, foresee, dominion, cogitator
