A step performed with the machine's own authority, from a sanction

Some steps reach beyond what their invoker may touch: the knobs of the
kernel beneath /sys, sysctl, the scriptures of /etc, the units of the
machine itself. Write "exalted": true upon such a step, and it is performed
not with its invoker's authority but with the machine's own, by an exalted
servitor raised for that one invocation — and only as its master sanctioned
it. Every other verse is performed as ever, with the invoker's authority.

Any step but a vox-cast may be exalted, in rites and edicts alike (an edict
exalts its incantations and litanies). "exalted" is true for every aspect or
for none, never an aspect map; false is as if unwritten.

The words of an exalted verse are fixed when its master sanctions them:
commands, reversions, vessels, anchors, offerings, and scripture inline or
illuminated from a tome. {{aspect}}, {{rite.name}} and an inscription taken
from its decrees may be spliced into them; {{former}} may not, nor a
mandatory inscription that some aspect does not decree, for their words are
given only at invocation. Exalted speech hears them from its environment
instead: "$SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT" and "$SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY>".

An exalted vessel or bound name must begin at the root once rendered, and
may not lie beneath /proc, /sys or /dev: the knobs of the kernel are written
by exalted incantations, never as vessels. Scriptures that exalted speech
reads beside its scroll are named among its relics.

No exalted verse is performed without the sanction of its master, laid with
servitor sanction <rite> and withdrawn with servitor abjure <rite>, and none
is ever performed by the servitor that invokes it. Invoked, the rite's
pre-flight compares the sigil of its exalted verses for the aspect with the
sanction: unsanctioned or lapsed, the invocation is refused before any proof
is asked, its death-mark 2, and the words name servitor sanction <rite>.
Sanctioned, the intercessor asks the master's proof once, after the
pre-flight, and raises one exalted servitor, which performs each exalted
verse in its turn among the common verses, from the sanctioned copy alone,
and reverts them, should the invocation fall, among the common reversions,
without a second proof. A proof dismissed touches nothing (death-mark 2,
no chronicle). The chronicle names the intercessor and the sigil.
--foresee reveals what it would do, marks each exalted verse ✠ and tells the
sanction standing, and never exalts. The cogitator lends no intercessor yet:
it refuses exalted verses, even sanctioned, before anything is touched —
invoke them at a terminal.

The inquisition notes the impurities of exaltation: sudo, run0, pkexec or
doas within words that are not exalted (write "exalted": true instead),
exalted speech reaching into the invoker's home ($HOME, ~) or for scripture
beside it that is not among its relics, and a program the machine guards by
an intercessor of its own (systemctl, hostnamectl, …) called by a verse that
is not exalted beside exalted ones.

Kindred passages: sanction, abjure, sigil, relics, intercessor, exalted-servitor,
incantation, litany, transcription, invoke, foresee, dominion

Exempla:
  {"incantation": {"on": "sysctl -wq kernel.split_lock_mitigate=0",
                   "*":  "sysctl -wq kernel.split_lock_mitigate=1"},
   "exalted": true}

  {"transcription": "/etc/sysctl.d/90-{{rite.name}}.conf", "exalted": true,
   "scripture": "kernel.split_lock_mitigate = 0"}
