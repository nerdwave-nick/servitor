The place of a verse within an edict's liturgy, counted from one

The verses of an edict's liturgy are named by their place: the first written
is verse 1, the next verse 2, and so on to the last. Wherever the servitor
speaks of one verse, it names its place and then its designation and what it
holds — never a bare number, but as the liturgy itself would say it. A rite
of aspects keeps no liturgy, and names each subject and observance by its
"name" instead: "subject smt · machine-spirit", "observance veil ·
incantation".

The chronicle names the verse of an edict that fell by its place and its
designation, "fell_at": {"verse": 2, "incantation": "…"}, and each reversion by its verse and verdict, as it names a
subject or observance of a rite of aspects by {"subject": …} or
{"observance": …}. On the terminal, foresight, reports and laments speak
"verse 1 · incantation grim …". The vox-casts of an edict's progress tell
how many of its verses are performed, and their tidings may name the verse
just performed as {{step}}.

The heresies of a verse are found by line and column within the scripture,
not by verse; the verse names it in what the servitor performed.

In the elder pattern "Mark I" every rite kept a liturgy of steps, each a
verse, vox-casts among them, and omens, taints and the chronicle named a
step by its verse and its own key: {"verse": 2, "tether": "…"}. Conversion
names each subject and observance it makes after the verse it was:
"tether-2", "incantation-1-on" (see conversion).

Kindred passages: liturgy, edict, chronicle, verdicts, foresee, conversion

Exempla:
  servitor invoke screenshot --foresee

  verse 1 · incantation grim -g "$(slurp)" ~/pictures/visage.png
  verse 2 · incantation wl-copy --type image/png < ~/pictures/visage.png

  "fell_at": {"verse": 2, "incantation": "wl-copy --type image/png < …"}
