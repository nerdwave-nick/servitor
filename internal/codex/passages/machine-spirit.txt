A subject read by its auspex and set by its enact words

A machine-spirit is the designation of a subject that no vessel or link
reveals: a knob of the kernel, a daemon awakened or slain, the mode of a
herald of the desktop. Its "omens" give the words it shows in each aspect,
its "auspex" reads which of them it shows now, and its "enact" words set it
into an omen.

Without "omens" each aspect's own name is its omen, so a scan that prints
the aspect itself serves. An omen shared by several aspects fits all of
them. A machine-spirit without "enact" is heresy, for a subject that cannot
be set belongs in no rite; one without an "auspex" cannot be read, so it is
enacted on every invocation and never proven, which the inquisition notes
as an impurity.

Its words are spoken in its "tongue", else the rite's, else the settings',
else bash, with its "patience" (30s, as the settings say, when unwritten).
Should it be exalted, its enact words are spoken with the machine's own
authority from a sanction, and may read its "relics".

Kindred passages: designation, enact, auspex, scan, trials, omens, subjects,
exalted, relics

   "auspex": {"scan": "sysctl -n kernel.split_lock_mitigate"},
   "enact": {"*": "sysctl -wq kernel.split_lock_mitigate={{omen}}"}}
