The mark that seals a sanction, drawn from every word of a rite's exalted verses

A sigil is drawn from the sanction itself: everything the rite's exalted
verses do, written in one canonical form (RFC 8785) and reduced by SHA-256
to sixty-four letters. The same verses always yield the same sigil, and any
change to them — a word of an exalted command or reversion, a vessel, an
anchor, a seal, the tongue, the patience, a scroll, a relic, a tome or the
decree of an inscription spliced into them — yields another.

A rite bears one sigil for all its exalted verses, and one more for each
aspect: the sigil of the sanction narrowed to that aspect alone. An edict,
which bears no aspect, has but one. Common verses touch no sigil.

A rite whose sigil no longer matches the sigil of its sanction has lapsed,
and must be sanctioned anew before its exalted verses are performed. The
first twelve letters name the sigil in the list of sanctions, in the proof
the master is asked for and in the chronicle; the binharic list speaks it
whole, under the key "sigil".

Kindred passages: sanction, exalted, relics

Exempla:
  servitor sanction
  servitor sanction --binharic | jq -r '.[] | "\(.rite) \(.sigil)"'
