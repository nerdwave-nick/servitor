The mark that seals a sanction, drawn from every word a rite holds exalted

A sigil is drawn from the sanction itself: everything the rite's exalted
subjects, observances or verses do, written in one canonical form (RFC 8785)
and reduced by SHA-256 to sixty-four letters. The same exalted words always
yield the same sigil, and any change to them — a word of an exalted command or
reversion, a vessel, an anchor, a seal, the tongue, the patience, a scroll, a
relic, a tome or the decree of an inscription spliced into them — yields
another.

A rite bears one sigil for everything it holds exalted, and one more for each
aspect: the sigil of the sanction narrowed to that aspect alone. An edict,
which bears no aspect, has but one. Common subjects, observances and verses
touch no sigil.

A rite whose sigil no longer matches the sigil of its sanction has lapsed, and
must be sanctioned anew before anything it holds exalted is performed. The
first twelve letters name the sigil in the list of sanctions, in the proof the
master is asked for and in the chronicle; the binharic list speaks it whole,
under the key "sigil".

Kindred passages: sanction, exalted, relics

Exempla:
  servitor sanction
  servitor sanction --binharic | jq -r '.[] | "\(.rite) \(.sigil)"'
