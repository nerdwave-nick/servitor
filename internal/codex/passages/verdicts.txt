How an invocation ended: triumph, reverted or faltered

When the invocation is done, the servitor passes judgement upon the invocation,
and that verdict is inscribed on the rite's data-slate and in the chronicle.
Three verdicts are known to the codex:

  triumph    every changing subject was set and proven and every observance
             spoken — or nothing needed to change; the rite stands in the
             aspect invoked, or the edict is obeyed
  reverted   a subject, an observance or a verse fell, and everything
             already performed was undone; the machine stands as it stood
             before
  faltered   something fell, and at least one reversion fell too; the
             machine stands half in one aspect and half in another, or
             half touched by an edict

A reverted or faltered invocation ends with the servitor's lament — what fell
(for a subject, the omen commanded and the omen found), its last words, and how each reversion went — and the invoke
ritual bears the death-mark 1 when reverted and 3 when faltered (0 upon
triumph). Spoken with --binharic, it speaks the chronicle's line of the
invocation, verdict and all, upon stdout. A faltered verdict leaves the rite corrupted until it is
invoked anew, for no single aspect can be trusted to stand; an edict lies
corrupted alike, until it is proclaimed again.

The verdict is kept with its hour as "last_rite": {"verdict", "at"}, the
hour in UTC to the second. The augury and the census speak it for every
rite; it is null for a rite never invoked through the servitor. The
chronicle keeps the verdict of every invocation, with "fell_at" and
"reversions" for those that fell.

An invocation refused in its pre-flight performed nothing and earns no
verdict: neither its data-slate nor the chronicle is touched. So too an
invocation refused before any proof for the sanction of what it exalts, or
whose proof was dismissed (death-mark 2). What is exalted is judged with the
rest: the verdict tells its reversions among the common ones. The same holds
for an invocation only foreseen. An invocation halted by its master's
interrupt is reverted like any other that fell, and judged accordingly.

Kindred passages: chronicle, data-slate, reversion, invoke, standing,
death-marks, forbidden

Exempla:
  servitor augury theme | jq -r .last_rite.verdict
