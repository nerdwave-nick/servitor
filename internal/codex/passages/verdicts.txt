How an invocation ended: triumph, reverted or faltered

When the liturgy is done, the servitor passes judgement upon the invocation,
and that verdict is inscribed on the rite's data-slate and in the chronicle.
Three verdicts are known to the codex:

  triumph    every step was performed; the rite stands in the aspect invoked
  reverted   a step fell, and every step already performed was undone; the
             machine stands as it stood before
  faltered   a step fell, and at least one reversion fell too; the machine
             stands half in one aspect and half in another

A reverted or faltered invocation ends with the servitor's lament — the step
that fell, its last words, and how each reversion went — and the invoke
ritual exits 1. A faltered verdict leaves the rite corrupted until it is
invoked anew, for no single aspect can be trusted to stand.

The verdict is kept with its hour as "last_rite": {"verdict", "at"}, the
hour in UTC to the second. The augury and the census speak it for every
rite; it is null for a rite never invoked through the servitor. The
chronicle keeps the verdict of every invocation, with "fell_at" and
"reversions" for those that fell.

An invocation refused in its pre-flight performed nothing and earns no
verdict: neither its data-slate nor the chronicle is touched. The same holds
for an invocation only foreseen. An invocation halted by its master's
interrupt is reverted like any other that fell, and judged accordingly.

Kindred passages: chronicle, data-slate, reversion, invoke, standing

Exempla:
  servitor augury theme | jq -r .last_rite.verdict

   "former": "default", "inscriptions": {}, "verdict": "faltered",
   "fell_at": {"verse": 3, "incantation": "make-it-so"},
   "heresy": "…", "reversions": [{"verse": 2, "verdict": "faltered"}]}
