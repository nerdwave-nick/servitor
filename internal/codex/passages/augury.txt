Read how a rite stands, the aspect it bears, and its inscriptions

The augury reads the omens of one rite and speaks what they reveal: the
aspect the machine stands in, the rite's standing, whether other hands
desecrated it, the inscriptions of its last invocation, its last rite with
verdict and hour, the aspects its subjects agree upon, every omen with its
subject, and every taint upon its sanctums. The augury changes nothing; it
reads every subject and awakens the auspex of every machine-spirit — never
one marked exalted — but sets nothing. An edict, which stands in no aspect,
yields no omen: its augury is read from its data-slate alone.

Roused as root by sudo, the augury reads the rite of the invoker's Librarium,
with the invoker's vessels and data-slate, and writes nothing; the passage on
the Librarium tells all.

Without a key, the augury speaks one object in binharic, for other machines
to read:

"fitting" holds the aspects every omen fits ([] when they fit none, or when
no subject yielded an omen). Each omen is named by its subject and
designation, with "omen" — the words a machine-spirit showed, when it showed
one declared omen — "aspects", every aspect it fits, and "aspect" when it
fits but one; a subject that yields no omen is not listed. The sanctums of
"taint" are named alike ({"subject": "cursor", "designation": "sanctum"}).
An omen taken from the data-slate's record, where the live omen of its
subject was not heard, bears "stale": true and "read_at", the hour it was
read; the augury awaits every live auspex, so only an exalted auspex, or one
spared by --spare-auspex, is ever told stale. The passage on the omens
tells all.

The key "former", naming the aspect the data-slate recorded, appears only
when the rite is desecrated. The key "auspex" tells what the auspexes of its
machine-spirits gave: "" when none keeps one the augury may speak,
"heeded", "mute", or "forgone" when the rune --spare-auspex left them
unawakened; the passage on the auspex tells all. The key "sanction" tells
how the rite stands towards the sanction of its exalted subjects,
observances or verses: "sanctioned", "lapsed", "unsanctioned", or "" for a
rite that holds nothing exalted; the passage on the sanction tells all. The
key "canon" holds every inscription the rite declares, in written order,
with its purpose, whether it is mandatory and its decrees; "inscriptions"
holds the words the data-slate keeps. A heretical rite's canon is [], and
the passage on the canon tells all.

The augury of an edict bears "edict": true and only its standing, its
sanction, its auspex (always "", for it keeps none), its inscriptions, its canon and its
last rite — never aspect, desecrated, former, omens or taint:

              "mandatory": false, "decrees": {}, "decree": "full"}],

With a key, the augury speaks that single value and nothing more: aspect,
standing, desecrated, former, sanction, or any inscription the rite declares
(an empty line when it is uninscribed). Should an inscription bear the name
of one of the first five, the augury's own value is spoken; the inscription
is still read in the binharic object. An edict answers its standing, its
sanction and its inscriptions alone: asked for its aspect, desecrated or former, it is refused
with a heresy, for it bears no aspect.

With the rune --spare-auspex, the augury awakens no auspex and answers at
once from the other omens, the omens the data-slate recorded and the
data-slate itself, in every manner of reading.

With the rune --is <aspect>, the augury speaks nothing at all: it exits 0
when the rite stands in that aspect and 1 when it does not, for the
conditions of other rites and commands. An edict bears no aspect to ask
after, and --is upon an edict is refused with a heresy.

The augury exits 0 when it was read — a dormant or heretical rite is still
read and spoken — 1 when --is did not match, and 2 when it could not be read:
an unknown rite or key, an aspect the rite knows not, a key given beside
--is, a heretical rite asked --is, an edict asked for an aspect, desecrated,
former or --is, or a malformed rune.

Kindred passages: is, spare-auspex, omens, standing, data-slate,
inscriptions, canon, auspex, subjects, edict, sanction

Exempla:
  servitor augury theme
  servitor augury theme aspect
  servitor augury theme reason
  servitor augury theme | jq -r .inscriptions.reason
  servitor augury theme | jq -r '.canon[].key'
  servitor augury mouse-autohide-toggle --is on && echo "the cursor is veiled"
  servitor augury screenshot standing
  servitor augury split-lock sanction
  servitor augury do-not-disturb --spare-auspex
