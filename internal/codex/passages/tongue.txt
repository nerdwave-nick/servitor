The program in which incantations and litanies are spoken

The tongue is the language the servitor speaks its commands in: the program
to which an incantation's words, a reversion and the auspex are handed as
"<tongue> -c <words>", and through which a litany's scroll is recited when
the scroll cannot be executed by its own shebang. Unless commanded otherwise
the servitor speaks bash.

A tongue is named by one word alone: the name or path of a single program,
sought along the machine's PATH. No words may follow it — "bash -e" names a
program that does not exist. Should you need such words, speak them inside
the incantation itself ("set -e; …").

The tongue may be named in three places, and the nearest prevails: on the
step (incantations and litanies), on the rite (for every step of its liturgy
and for its auspex), and in the settings (for every rite of the Librarium).
Where none is named, bash is spoken.

Before anything is performed, the pre-flight seeks every tongue the
invocation will speak; a tongue spoken nowhere on this machine refuses the
invocation, and the inquisition notes it as an impurity. A litany that is
recited by its own shebang needs no tongue, unless it carries a reversion.

Kindred passages: incantation, litany, reversion, auspex, patience

Exempla:
  // on a step
  {"incantation": "print -P '%F{red}{{aspect}}%f'", "tongue": "zsh"}

  // on a rite, for its whole liturgy and its auspex
  {"pattern": "Mark I", "aspects": ["on", "off"], "tongue": "dash",
   "auspex": "test -e /run/user/1000/veiled && echo on || echo off",
   "liturgy": [{"incantation": "touch /run/user/1000/veiled"}]}

  // in servitor.json, for the whole Librarium
  {"pattern": "Mark I", "tongue": "/usr/bin/fish"}
