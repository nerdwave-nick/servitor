A step that recites a whole written scroll of commands

Where an incantation speaks a single line, a litany recites a whole scroll:
a scripture-file of commands kept beside the rite or anywhere upon the
machine. The step's own key holds the path of the scroll, and it may differ
for every aspect as an aspect map. A leading "~" and the $NAMES of the
environment are expanded, and a path that does not begin at the root is
reckoned from the hall where the rite's scripture is kept.

A scroll the machine may execute is recited by its own shebang, whatever its
tongue; any other scroll is recited through the tongue of the step, the rite
or the settings ("<tongue> <scroll>"). The scroll must stand when its turn
comes: the pre-flight looks for it as the steps before it would leave the
machine, so a transcription earlier in the liturgy may write the very scroll
a litany then recites.

A litany receives nothing it is not offered. Under "offerings" a list of
words is laid before the scroll, each handed over exactly as written — never
parsed by a tongue, never split, no $NAME expanded — with only its
placeholders filled and a leading "~/" borne to the invoker's home; each may
differ per aspect. Without offerings the scroll is recited bare, and learns
the aspect from SERVITOR_ASPECT and its kin.

Like an incantation, the scroll is recited in the rite's hall with nothing
upon its input, anointed with SERVITOR_ASPECT, SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT,
SERVITOR_RITE and SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY> — the scroll of an edict with
SERVITOR_RITE and SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY> alone, for an edict bears no
aspect. A death-mark other than 0, or patience run out (30s unless the
settings or the step say otherwise), makes it fall; on running out the
servitor slays it with every process it summoned. Its words are captured,
never printed. Background children that must outlive it should send their
words elsewhere ("cmd >/dev/null 2>&1 &"). Interactive scrolls are
unsupported: neither the cogitator nor a hotkey lends them a terminal.

Its "reversion" is not a scroll but one line of words, spoken in the tongue
should the invocation fall. A litany yields no omen.

An exalted litany is recited by the exalted servitor from the scroll its
master sanctioned, never from the scroll as it stands now: the scroll is
laid in a working hall of its own beside its relics, under the name it is
written by (or its last name, when it lies beyond the rite's hall). A scroll
whose first line names an interpreter ("#!/usr/bin/bash -e") is recited by
that interpreter, which must be a program of the root like any tongue;
every other through its tongue, bidden to read no scripture of its own
(bash, zsh, fish, sh or dash alone; see tongue). It hears
PATH=/usr/local/sbin:/usr/local/bin:/usr/bin, HOME=/root, the locale and the
SERVITOR_ variables alone. What it leaves behind is slain when the
invocation ends; a root process meant to last belongs in a unit of the
machine ("systemd-run --unit=…").

Kindred passages: offerings, incantation, tongue, patience, reversion,
placeholders, relics, exalted

Exempla:
  {"litany": "scripts/change-theme",
   "offerings": ["{{aspect}}", "{{former}}"]}

  {"litany": {"day": "hooks/dawn.sh", "night": "hooks/dusk.sh"},
   "tongue": "zsh",
   "patience": "1m",
   "reversion": "hooks/restore.sh \"$SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT\""}
