A rite without aspects, proclaimed whole each time it is invoked

Some rites bring the machine into no aspect at all: they are commands done
once and done again — the visage of the machine captured, the machine spirit
bidden to sleep or to wake anew. Such a rite is written as an edict, marked
by "edict": true in place of "aspects". "edict": false is as if unwritten.
A rite bears exactly one of the two: "aspects" beside "edict": true is
heresy, and so is a rite that bears neither.

An edict is invoked by its name alone, with no aspect after it, and every
invocation performs its whole liturgy anew; nothing is passed over because
it was performed before, for there is nothing to read. Its inscriptions are
runes as for any rite.

Its "liturgy" is a list of verses in the designation form: "designation":
"incantation" with its "words", or "designation": "litany" with its
"scroll" and "offerings", each with its "tongue", "patience", "reversion",
"exalted" and "relics". Verses bear no name; they are named by their place,
counted from one as written. Its vox-casts are no verses but the rite's own
list under "vox-cast": "progress" tells of each verse once performed,
"success" of the whole edict obeyed.

Because an edict stands in no aspect, nothing of it may hold or reveal one:
  - it bears no subjects and no observances, for each exists to keep an
    aspect or to be spoken as one changes
  - it bears no auspex, for there is no aspect to divine
  - every value is written once and plainly: an aspect map — even one keyed
    by "*" alone — is heresy, and its decrees are one plain word
  - its words never speak {{aspect}} or {{former}}; {{rite.name}} and
    {{inscription.<key>}} serve it as they serve every rite

All else is as for any rite: "purpose", "inscriptions" with "mandatory" and
a decree of one plain word, "tongue", and the reversion, patience, tongue and
offerings of its verses. Should a verse fall, the verses performed are
reverted in reverse order, each by its own reversion words. Should the
servitor write an edict's scripture anew, it keeps "edict": true and never
writes "aspects".

An edict may exalt its verses: the exalted servitor, roused with no aspect,
performs them from the sanction of its master, one proof asked at every
proclaiming, as the passage on exaltation tells. Its sanction holds one
plan, whose sigil is that of the whole unit.

An edict yields no omen, so it is judged by the verdict of its last
invocation alone: dormant until first proclaimed, proclaimed after a triumph
or a reversion, corrupted after a faltered invocation until it is proclaimed
again. Its augury speaks its standing, inscriptions and last rite, and never
an aspect; the census marks it "edict": true, with no aspects. The
Inquisition weighs its scrolls and tongues once, as it bears no aspect.

An edict of the elder pattern "Mark I" wrote its incantations, litanies and
vox-casts as steps, each by its own key; conversion gives each verse the
designation form and gathers the vox-casts into the list (see conversion).

Kindred passages: rite, aspects, liturgy, incantation, litany, vox-cast,
invoke, heresy, standing, augury, census, exalted, conversion
