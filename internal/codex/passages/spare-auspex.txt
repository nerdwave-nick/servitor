Answer at once, leaving every auspex unawakened

The rune --spare-auspex is given to the census and to the augury. Without
it, both awaken the auspex of every rite that keeps one, and each auspex may
labour as long as its patience allows before the answer is spoken.

With the rune, no auspex is ever awakened. The answer is read from the omens
of the steps and from the data-slate alone, and so it comes at once — fit for
bars and other lesser machines that ask often and cannot wait. The aspect
and the standing are still judged by the agreement of whatever omens remain;
where none speaks, the data-slate names the aspect, as ever. A spared
machine-spirit of pattern Mark II is read from the omen the data-slate
recorded at the last invocation, marked stale, when that omen was read in
this boot of the machine and is still declared.

Such an answer is no full reading, and it says so: every rite that keeps an
auspex is marked "auspex": "forgone" in the binharic of the census and of
the augury. A rite without auspex, an edict and a heretical rite are marked
"" as ever, for there was nothing to spare.

The rune changes only what is read, never what is spoken: the census table
stays as it is, and the augury's keys and --is answer as without it. The
litanies of completion spare the auspex always, and so does --foresee.

Given with --vigil, the census tells every rite once, whole, in its first
telling — none is awaited, for no auspex will answer — and concludes at
once.

Kindred passages: auspex, census, augury, vigil, omens, data-slate, patience

Exempla:
  servitor census --binharic --spare-auspex
  servitor census --vigil --spare-auspex
  servitor augury do-not-disturb --spare-auspex | jq -r .aspect
  servitor augury do-not-disturb --spare-auspex --is on && echo "silence reigns"
