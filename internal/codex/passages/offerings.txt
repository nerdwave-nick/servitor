What is laid before a litany for its scroll to work with

A litany receives nothing it is not offered. Its "offerings" are a list of
words handed to the scroll in the order written, each as one whole word: the
servitor neither splits them at spaces, nor reads quotation marks, nor lets
any tongue interpret them, nor expands a $NAME. Their placeholders are filled
— {{aspect}}, {{former}}, {{rite.name}} and {{inscription.<key>}}, though an
edict's never hold {{aspect}} or {{former}} — so an inscription full of spaces
and quotation marks arrives at the scroll exactly as it was given.

One mercy alone is granted, as to the path of a vessel: an offering that,
once its placeholders are filled, begins with "~/" — or is "~" and nothing
more — is offered from the home of the invoker. Any other "~" stays as
written, and an offering is never reckoned from the rite's hall.

Every offering is written plainly, once, never as an aspect map; offerings
that differ by aspect are laid before several observances guarded by "to". Without offerings the scroll is recited bare,
and must learn what it needs from the environment (SERVITOR_ASPECT and its
kin; for an edict, SERVITOR_RITE and its inscriptions).

Offerings belong to litanies alone — observances and verses of an edict.
An incantation's words are handed whole to its tongue instead, and need no
offerings.

Kindred passages: litany, placeholders, aspect-maps, inscriptions
