The named conditions a rite can bring the machine into

Under "aspects" a rite lists, by hand and in order, every condition it can
bring the machine into: "on" and "off", "dark" and "light", the names of the
themes of a visage. The list is never discovered from halls or vessels and
never asked of the faithful while the rite is performed; what is not written
here does not exist for the rite.

The list must not be empty, for a rite without aspects can never be invoked,
and no aspect may be named twice. An aspect's name must begin with a letter
or digit and continue only with letters, digits, ".", "_" or "-"; the "*"
is no aspect but the fallback of aspect maps, serving every aspect not named
there.

Completion offers the aspects in the order written, and marks the one the
rite stands in. Every aspect map of the rite's steps — scripture, anchors,
commands, scrolls, offerings, reversions — must hold a value for every
aspect, written by name or served by "*"; only decrees may leave an aspect
without. An aspect-map key that names no declared aspect is heresy.

At invocation the aspect being invoked is spoken as {{aspect}} and in the
environment as SERVITOR_ASPECT; the aspect the rite stood in before is
{{former}} and SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT. Which aspect a rite stands in now is
divined from its omens, as the augury tells.

Kindred passages: aspect-maps, invoke, augury, omens, standing, placeholders

Exempla:
  "aspects": ["default", "finii", "porpl"]

  servitor invoke theme porpl
  servitor augury theme --is porpl && echo "the visage is porpl"
