Sanction the exalted verses of a rite with one proof, or list the sanctions and how each stands

Nothing exalted is performed but as its master sanctioned it. A sanction is
a copy of what a rite exalts — its exalted subjects and observances, or an
edict's exalted verses — laid where only the machine's own authority writes,
sealed by its sigil:

  /var/lib/servitor/sanctions/<uid>/<rite>.json

It holds everything those verses do, for every aspect (once for an edict):
their verses with their words and reversions rendered, the tongue as the
whole path of its program, their patience, the words of every scroll, relic,
tome and transcribed scripture, seal and zeal, the decree of every
inscription spliced into them, and the names of the variables their speech
hears. Common verses are no part of it: changing a vox-cast, or an
incantation that is not exalted, changes no sanction.

A rite of aspects of pattern Mark II is sanctioned by its exalted subjects
and observances, and its sanction bears the pattern Mark II, so that
converting a rite lapses it. For every aspect it holds the exalted subjects
in written order, each with its place among the rite's subjects: a sanctum,
transcription or tether resolved as an exalted verse is; a machine-spirit by
the omen of that aspect, its tongue, patience and relics, and its enact
words for every omen it declares, for a reversion enacts the omen the
subject was read to show, which belongs to another aspect. Then the exalted
observances that aspect admits by "to", each with its "from" and the places
of the subjects that name it. So the enact words of an exalted
machine-spirit lapse every aspect when they change; an observance guarded
by "to" lapses only the aspects it admits; and a common subject or
observance, or the common auspex of an exalted subject, lapses nothing. An
auspex itself marked "exalted" is part of the sanction, in every aspect
that holds its subject: its scan, its trial for every declared omen, its
patience, and the tongue and relics of its subject; editing any of them
lapses every such aspect. The census and the augury tell its standing as
they tell any rite's.

Spoken alone, the ritual lists every sanction laid for you, by rite: its
standing, its sigil, and the hour it was sanctioned. Spoken plainly it is a
table with the columns RITE, STANDING, SIGIL (its first twelve letters) and
SANCTIONED AT; with --binharic a list of objects bearing rite, standing,
sigil (whole) and sanctioned_at. The list only reads, needs no proof of the
master, and exits 0.

Every rite stands towards its sanction in one of these ways, which the
census and the augury tell under the key "sanction":

  sanctioned    ✠  its exalted verses are those its master sanctioned
  lapsed        ☓  they changed since, or can no longer be read whole
  unsanctioned  ○  it holds exalted verses, yet no sanction
  forsaken      †  a sanction whose rite is gone, or holds no exalted verse
  ""               the rite exalts nothing and needs no sanction

The glyphs mark the rites of the census table and the sanctions of the
list. A forsaken sanction belongs to no rite of the census: the census
laments it apart, and the list names it. A heretical rite whose sanction
is kept has lapsed.

Spoken with a rite, the ritual sanctions its exalted verses. The
Inquisition comes first: a rite holding any heresy is refused, and so is a
rite unrecorded or holding no exalted verse, for there is nothing to
sanction — should a sanction still be kept under its name, the words say it
is forsaken and how it is abjured. Then the servitor shows its master the
whole of what would be sanctioned, every aspect under its own sigil and the
whole under one, every exalted verse with every word, path, seal, tongue,
patience, scroll, relic and scripture it holds; or, when a sanction stands,
only what changed since: the lines it lost marked "-", those it gained "+",
amid a few that stayed, beneath the sigil it bore and the sigil it bears
now. Scripture that is not words, or bears signs a terminal would heed
rather than show, is told by its size and SHA-256 alone. When nothing
changed, the servitor says so and stops, its death-mark 0, asking no proof.

The master must behold what they sanction: without a terminal (or the
cogitator) the sanctioning is refused, its death-mark 2. Otherwise the
intercessor asks for the master's proof, once, and that proof is the
sanction itself — no other question is asked. The exalted servitor it
raises hears the unit, checks its sigil against the words that roused it,
judges it by the rules of every invocation, and lays it in the store: on
first use it raises /var/lib/servitor and sanctions/, the root's and sealed
0755, and <uid>/, the root's and of the master's group, sealed 0750; the
sanction itself is the root's and of the master's group, sealed 0640, strict
JSON, written whole beside its place and set there at once, so that no
reader ever finds half of it. It journals the master's uid, the rite, the
sigil and the sigil it replaced. A proof dismissed or failed ends the
sanctioning with the death-mark 1, and nothing is written; an intercessor
nowhere to be found, a servitor installed amiss or of another forging, and
everything the exalted servitor refuses bear 2, and nothing is written. The
sanctioning knows no binharic: spoken with --binharic, it is refused with 2.

Roused as root the ritual is refused in every form, its death-mark 2:
sanctions are the master's, laid and read as themselves.

The inquisition notes as impurities an exalted rite that stands unsanctioned
or whose sanction lapsed, and every forsaken sanction; --spare-vessels
spares them.

Kindred passages: abjure, sigil, exalted, relics, intercessor, exalted-servitor, census, augury,
inquisition, invoke

Exempla:
  servitor sanction split-lock
  servitor sanction
  servitor sanction --binharic | jq -r '.[] | select(.standing != "sanctioned") | .rite'
  servitor augury split-lock sanction
