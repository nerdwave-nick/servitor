List the sanctions of exalted verses, and how each rite stands towards its own

No exalted verse is performed but as its master sanctioned it. A sanction is
a copy of a rite's exalted verses laid where only the machine's own
authority writes, sealed by its sigil:

  /var/lib/servitor/sanctions/<uid>/<rite>.json

It holds everything those verses do, for every aspect (once for an edict):
their verses with their words and reversions rendered, the tongue as the
whole path of its program, their patience, the words of every scroll, relic,
tome and transcribed scripture, seal and zeal, the decree of every
inscription spliced into them, and the names of the variables their speech
hears. Common verses are no part of it: changing a vox-cast, or an
incantation that is not exalted, changes no sanction.

Spoken alone, the ritual lists every sanction laid for you, by rite: its
standing, its sigil, and the hour it was sanctioned. Spoken plainly it is a
table with the columns RITE, STANDING, SIGIL (its first twelve letters) and
SANCTIONED AT; with --binharic a list of objects bearing rite, standing,
sigil (whole) and sanctioned_at. The list only reads, needs no proof of the
master, and exits 0.

Every rite stands towards its sanction in one of these ways, which the
census and the augury tell under the key "sanction":

  sanctioned    ✠  its exalted verses are those its master sanctioned
  lapsed        ☓  they changed since, or can no longer be read whole
  unsanctioned  ○  it holds exalted verses, yet no sanction
  forsaken      †  a sanction whose rite is gone, or holds no exalted verse
  ""               the rite holds no exalted verse and needs no sanction

The glyphs mark the rites of the census table and the sanctions of the
list. A forsaken sanction belongs to no rite of the census: the census
laments it apart, and the list names it. A heretical rite whose sanction
is kept has lapsed.

Spoken with a rite, the ritual sanctions its exalted verses with one proof
of the master; this servitor cannot yet lay a sanction, and refuses it with
the death-mark 2, writing nothing. Roused as root the ritual is refused in
every form, its death-mark 2: sanctions are the master's, laid and read as
themselves.

The inquisition notes as impurities an exalted rite that stands unsanctioned
or whose sanction lapsed, and every forsaken sanction; --spare-vessels
spares them.

Kindred passages: sigil, exalted, relics, census, augury, inquisition, invoke

Exempla:
  servitor sanction
  servitor sanction --binharic | jq -r '.[] | select(.standing != "sanctioned") | .rite'
  servitor augury split-lock sanction
