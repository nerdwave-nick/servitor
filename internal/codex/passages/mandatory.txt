Whether an invocation may proceed without the word of an inscription

An inscription declared "mandatory": true must bear a word in every
invocation of its rite. The word may come from the rune --<key> given to the
invocation, or from the rite's decree for the invoked aspect; when neither
supplies one — or when the rune clears the decree with an empty word — the
invocation is refused as heresy before a single step is performed, and the
lament names the inscription and the rune that would have satisfied it.

Unless written, "mandatory" is false: the inscription may be left
uninscribed, and it then illuminates as nothing and is kept on the data-slate
as absent.

The inquisition looks ahead on behalf of the faithful: a mandatory
inscription for which some aspect holds no decree is noted as an impurity,
for every invocation into that aspect will demand the rune each time. Such a
rite is not wrong — it may be a deliberate demand for a reason every time it
is invoked — but the servitor will have it known.

Kindred passages: inscriptions, decrees, invoke, inquisition, pre-flight

Exempla:
  "inscriptions": {
    "mode": {"mandatory": true, "decrees": {"on": "auto-hide", "off": "always-visible"}},
    "reason": {"mandatory": true, "purpose": "why the silence was imposed"}
  }

  servitor invoke do-not-disturb on --reason "the forge demands focus"
