The rite's list of tidings it sends of progress and of triumph

A rite's "vox-cast" names which tidings of an invocation it sends its master,
as a list of "progress" and "success", each at most once and in any order.
Without the key, only a fall is proclaimed. Any other value, or one given
twice, is heresy.

  "progress"  a rite of aspects tells of each subject once it is set and
              proven, counting "subject x / n" among the subjects that
              change; an edict tells of each verse once it is performed
  "success"   proclaims the triumph of the rite and the aspect it now
              bears — even when no subject needed to change — or that an
              edict is obeyed

Observances are not counted, and proclaim nothing of their own. Tidings of
failure need no word: should an invocation fall, the servitor proclaims the
heresy, what fell and how the reversion went, unbidden — for a subject, the
omen commanded and the omen found. The words themselves are drawn at random
from the servitor's own litanies of progress, triumph and lament, and speak
of the aspect, written «aspect», and of the subject by its name; a rite does
not write them. An edict's vox-casts are drawn from litanies of their own,
which name "the edict" and its name and never speak an aspect:

  ✠ The edict screenshot is proclaimed, and the machine obeys.

Where the tidings are heard depends on whence the servitor was roused. With
a controlling terminal, each is told in its place within the report of the
invocation (see invoke): a progress as a line of its own, ending in the count
of subjects done, and the triumph as the report's closing line. A fall is
told by the lament alone.

    ✔ observance veil · incantation
    ✔ subject visage · tether ~/.local/share/nfluff/current-theme
    ⋯ Cogs turn: visage now bears the aspect «porpl».  (1/1)
    ✔ observance awaken · litany
  ✠ Every rune turned, every litany sung: the aspect «porpl» prevails.

Without one — invoked from a hotkey or a launcher — every tiding of the
invocation becomes one missive upon the desktop through notify-send, headed by
the rite and its aspect ("theme → porpl"), or by an edict's name alone
("screenshot"), and replaced in place by the next: progress tells "subject
x / n" and lingers until the rite ends, triumph fades as the desktop wills,
and a fall is proclaimed with critical urgency so that it cannot pass unseen.
Should notify-send be absent or fall silent, the servitor stays silent too; a
vox-cast never fails an invocation. The settings' "vox" may send the tidings
of a terminal's invocations to the desktop as well ("notify-send"), or silence
the desktop altogether ("off"); when foreseeing, nothing is proclaimed at all.

Kindred passages: vox, invoke, verdicts, edict, subjects, conversion

Exempla:
  "vox-cast": ["progress", "success"]

  // only the triumph, for a hotkey that shows no terminal
  "vox-cast": ["success"]
