The undoing of a fallen invocation: restoring what was read, and undoing words

Should an invocation fall — a subject that cannot be set, a proof that finds
another omen than the one commanded, an observance or a verse whose
death-mark is not 0, a patience run out, or the invocation halted at its
master's command — the servitor does not leave the machine half-changed. It
reverts: everything already performed is undone in reverse order, the
fallen one included, for it may have done half its work.

A rite of aspects needs no words to undo its subjects: it restores what it
read. A sanctum, a transcribed vessel and a tether are written back exactly
as the servitor found them before it changed them — a sanctum's vessel and a
transcribed vessel to their former scripture and seal (a vessel the
invocation brought into being is struck again), a tether rebound to its
former anchor, or unbound. A machine-spirit is enacted into the omen it was
read to show before it was set, and proven by a reading afresh; one that
showed no omen — its auspex mute, or none — is enacted into the omen of the
former aspect. When the former aspect too is unknown, it cannot be restored:
that reversion falls, and the verdict is faltered. A restoration whose proof
finds another omen falls alike.

Observances, and the verses of an edict, cannot be undone by the servitor
alone; it does not know what their words did. Under "reversion" each may
carry the words that undo it — one line, written once and plainly, spoken in
its tongue, under its patience, in the rite's hall, with the same
environment as its own words; SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT (and {{former}}) name
the aspect the machine returns to, and are empty for an edict, which knows
no former aspect. Each spoken observance speaks its reversion in its place
among the restorations. One that bears none reverts nothing.

Exalted subjects, observances and verses are reverted by the exalted servitor
alone, in their place among the common reversions: the servitor bids it
revert each in its turn, naming the omen a machine-spirit is to be restored
to, and no second proof of the master is asked. The exalted servitor speaks
only restoring words its master sanctioned, restores an exalted vessel or
tether from what it kept itself, and proves a subject read by an exalted
auspex by itself. Should the servitor that invoked fall silent before the
end — slain, or its channel lost — the exalted servitor reverts everything it
performed by itself, from the last back to the first, and journals it.

A reversion that falls does not stop the others; every one is attempted and
its outcome told. When all succeed the verdict is reverted; when any falls,
the verdict is faltered, and the rite — or the edict — will likely show
corrupted. A halt from the terminal never halts the reversions. The
chronicle and the laments name each subject and observance reverted by its
name, and each verse of an edict by its place. When foreseeing, the
reversion each observance and verse would speak is shown.

In the elder pattern "Mark I" every incantation and litany carried its own
reversion words, which might differ per aspect. Conversion keeps them upon
the observances it makes, and strikes them from a machine-spirit, which is
restored by enacting the omen it showed (see conversion).

Kindred passages: verdicts, observances, machine-spirit, liturgy,
placeholders, chronicle, invoke, exalted-servitor

Exempla:
  // an observance that must be undone should a later subject fall
  {"name": "wlsunset", "designation": "incantation",
   "words": "systemctl --user stop wlsunset",
   "reversion": "systemctl --user start wlsunset"}

  {"name": "awaken", "designation": "litany", "scroll": "hooks/apply-theme",
   "offerings": ["{{aspect}}"],
   "reversion": "hooks/apply-theme \"$SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT\""}

  // in an edict: the words that undo it stand on their own
  {"designation": "incantation", "words": "systemctl --user stop syncthing",
   "reversion": "systemctl --user start syncthing"}
