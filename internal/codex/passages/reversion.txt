The undoing of a fallen invocation, and the words that undo a command

Should a step fall — its death-mark not 0, its patience run out, or the
invocation halted at its master's command — the servitor does not leave the
machine half-changed. It reverts: every step already performed is undone in
reverse order, from the fallen step back to the first, so that the machine
returns to its former aspect — or, for an edict, to how it stood before the
edict was proclaimed.

Sanctums, transcriptions and tethers revert themselves, for the servitor
captured what stood before it changed them: a sanctum and a transcribed
vessel are written back as they were (a vessel the step consecrated is
removed again), and a tether is rebound to its former anchor, or unbound.

Incantations and litanies cannot be undone by the servitor alone; it does not
know what their words did. Under "reversion" such a step carries the words
that undo it — one line, spoken in the step's tongue, under the step's
patience, in the rite's hall, with the same environment as the step. They may
differ per aspect as an aspect map, and {{former}} (and
SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT) name the aspect the machine is to return to. An edict
knows no former aspect: its reversion is written once, plainly, and undoes by
its words alone. The reversion of the fallen step itself is spoken too, for it
may have done half its work. A step without reversion reverts nothing.

Exalted verses are reverted by the exalted servitor alone, in their place
among the common reversions, from the fallen verse back to the first: the
servitor bids it revert each in its turn, and no second proof of the master
is asked. The reversion of an exalted incantation or litany is spoken by the
exalted servitor, with the machine's authority, in its working hall and with
the environment of exalted speech; an exalted vessel or tether it restores
itself. Should the servitor that invoked fall silent before the end — slain,
or its channel lost — the exalted servitor reverts every verse it performed
by itself, from the last back to the first, and journals it.

A rite of pattern Mark II needs no reversion words: it restores what it read.
Every subject set is restored in reverse order, the fallen one too. A
sanctum, transcription or tether is written back as above. A machine-spirit
is enacted into the omen it was read to show before it was set, and proven
by a reading afresh; one that showed no omen — its auspex mute, or none —
is enacted into the omen of the former aspect. When the former aspect too
is unknown, it cannot be restored: that reversion falls, and the verdict is
faltered. A spoken observance speaks its own reversion words, if it bears
any, in its place in that reverse order, the fallen one too. The chronicle
and the laments name each subject and observance by its name.

A reversion that falls does not stop the others; every one is attempted and
its outcome told. When all succeed the verdict is reverted; when any falls,
the verdict is faltered, and the rite — or the edict — will likely show
corrupted. A halt from the terminal never halts the reversions. When
foreseeing, the reversion each step would speak is shown.

Kindred passages: verdicts, incantation, litany, placeholders, chronicle,
invoke

Exempla:
  {"incantation": "systemctl --user stop wlsunset",
   "reversion": "systemctl --user start wlsunset"}

  // aspects "sharp" and "soft": only leaving "sharp" needs undoing
  {"incantation": {"sharp": "hyprctl keyword decoration:rounding 0",
                   "soft": "hyprctl keyword decoration:rounding 12"},
   "reversion": {"sharp": "hyprctl reload", "*": ""}}

  {"litany": "hooks/apply-theme", "offerings": ["{{aspect}}"],
   "reversion": "hooks/apply-theme \"$SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT\""}

  // in an edict: the words that undo it stand on their own
  {"incantation": "systemctl --user stop syncthing",
   "reversion": "systemctl --user start syncthing"}
