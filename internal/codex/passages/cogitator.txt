Awaken the cogitator, the interactive shrine of rites

The cogitator is the servitor's shrine for those who would rather behold than
type. It is awakened by the ritual servitor cogitator, or by the servitor
alone when it stands at a terminal; without a terminal the servitor recites
the index of the codex instead.

On its left the cogitator lists every rite of the Librarium with the glyph
of its standing and the aspect it stands in — an edict, which stands in none,
with its standing alone, beside the mark ❢ of an edict; on its right it
reveals the chosen rite: its purpose, its aspects with the current one marked,
its standing, its last rite, its inscriptions, its subjects and observances,
its vox-casts and where its scripture is recorded. An edict shows its nature
in place of aspects, and its liturgy, verse by verse, for it reveals no omen.
Heretical rites are shown too, with the heresy that taints them. Scripture of
pattern Mark I is shown converted, and a toast tells each
conversion the cogitator writes (see conversion).

A rite of aspects shows its subjects, one line each: the mark of its omen, the glyph of its designation —
§ sanctum, ¶ transcription, ↣ tether, ⚙ machine-spirit — marked ✠ when
exalted, its name, and what it was read to show: the omen a machine-spirit
printed, or the vessel or bound name of the others. The mark is ✔ when its
omen fits the aspect the rite stands in, ✖ when it fits another, and the
aspects it fits are named beside it; · marks a subject that yields no omen,
with the reason: mute, read yet fitting no aspect; unread, bearing no auspex
that may be heard outside an invocation; awaited, its auspex still
labouring; forgone, its auspex left unawakened. A subject whose omen is
taken from the record of the data-slate, where no live omen is heard, is
told stale, dimmed, with the age of its reading, as "stale · read 2h ago".
When the omens of its subjects agree on no aspect, the rite lies
corrupted, and each subject that fits no aspect the most of them fit is
marked ✖ as breaking the agreement. Its observances follow, each with its
glyph, the aspects it is spoken to and from, and its words; then the
vox-casts the rite bears as its own, which an edict shows too. A rite bears
no auspex of its own: each machine-spirit bears its own.

The cogitator never waits upon an auspex to draw the shrine. It draws
every rite at once, as the first heralding of census --vigil tells it: each
subject whose auspex is yet to answer shows the omen the data-slate recorded
in this boot, stale, or none, awaited. The list marks an aspect that rests on a stale omen with "~", as
"~on", as the census marks it. Then the auspexes of every rite labour
together as the vigil awakens them, and each rite is drawn anew with its
live omens once its own have settled. A subject read by an exalted auspex
stays stale, for that auspex is spoken at invocation alone. Every reading
of the Librarium — r, the end of an invocation, the sealing of the wizard —
begins anew, and slays the auspexes still labouring for the reading before,
whose words are no longer heeded; retreating from the cogitator slays every
auspex still labouring.

The preview of such a rite tells, for every subject, the omen it was read to
show, the omen commanded and whether it changes, with the words that would
set it, and the observances that would be spoken. While it is invoked, the
running panel counts subject x / n of the changing subjects alone, names the
subject being set and the omen it is set to, and the observances spoken. The
lament of its fall names the subject or observance that fell, the omen
commanded, the omen read before and the omen found, its last words, and the
verdict upon every reversion.

A rite holding exalted subjects, observances or verses bears the mark of its
sanction beside its aspect, as the census marks it: ✠ sanctioned, ☓ lapsed,
○ unsanctioned; a rite whose sanction is forsaken bears †, and a rite that
exalts nothing and holds no sanction bears none. The chosen rite tells its
sanction beneath its standing — the sigil and the hour it was sanctioned, the
sigil it lapsed from, or what s does about it — and marks every exalted
subject, observance and verse ✠, as foresight, the running panel and the
lament of a fall do.

Roused as root, the servitor awakens no cogitator, for the cogitator performs,
and the servitor performs nothing with the machine's own authority: it refuses
with the death-mark 2. The census, the augury and the Inquisition still read.

From the shrine the faithful choose a rite (j and k, or the arrows; g and G
for the first and last), invoke it with enter, cycle it to its next aspect
with space, consecrate a new rite with n, amend one with e, replicate one
with c, excommunicate one with d, sanction what it holds exalted or abjure its
sanction with s, open its scripture in $EDITOR with o,
summon the Inquisition with i, hear the words of the last invocation with O,
read the chronicle with h, filter the rites with /, re-read the Librarium
with r, and retreat with q. The key ? opens the codex itself: above it stands
the cogitator's own catalogue of keys, below it the index of every passage;
enter recites the chosen passage, as servitor expound would.

An edict is never proclaimed on a single key, for it may cast the machine
down. Upon an edict, enter and space alike open its invoke screen, which
offers no aspect to choose: it asks first for the edict's inscriptions, when
it bears any, each written beforehand with its one plain decree, and then
asks whether the edict shall be proclaimed. A second enter proclaims it,
performing its whole liturgy anew; p foresees it, and esc retreats with
nothing performed. While it is proclaimed, its panel, the tidings of its
triumph and the lament of its fall name the edict alone, never a former
aspect and an aspect invoked.

The key h unrolls the chronicle across the whole screen: every invocation of
the current chronicle and of the one rotated before it, newest first, a line
each with its moment, its rite, the former aspect and the aspect invoked — or,
for an edict, the mark ❢ edict in their place — and its verdict. It opens
already filtered to the rite chosen in the list; / rewrites the filter, which
seeks the rite, either aspect and the verdict, or the word edict for every
proclaiming of an edict, and esc while writing casts the filter away so that
every entry is shown. enter reads the chosen entry whole: its inscriptions,
the verse it fell at and the heresy that felled it, and the verdict upon every
reversion. esc returns to the chronicle, and from there to the rites. The
cogitator reads the chronicle that the rune --chronicle, the environment or
the settings place, as every ritual does; it never amends or strikes an entry.

The consecration wizard, awakened by n, e and c, writes every rite in pattern
Mark II. At the Rite the faithful speak its name and purpose, choose its
nature — a rite of aspects, or an edict — and speak its aspects, the
inscriptions it bears — each with its purpose, whether it is mandatory, and
its decrees, spoken per aspect and always written keyed by aspect — and its
vox-casts, of progress and of success, each borne by the rite itself. An edict
is asked no aspects, and each of its inscriptions takes one plain decree. A
rite of aspects then passes four stations: the Rite, the Subjects, the
Observances, and the Seal.

At the Subjects, a adds a subject once its designation is chosen — sanctum,
transcription, tether or machine-spirit — enter amends the chosen subject, d
strikes it, and J and K move it down and up, for the subjects are read and set
in the order written. Each subject is written on pages: first its essence —
its name, its purpose and its own keys, such as the vessel, the ward or the
bound name, and whether it is exalted — then its further rites where it bears
them, and its omens upon one grid, a row for each aspect, where an aspect may
bear the omen of every other aspect, written once under "*". The omens of a
machine-spirit may be left unwritten, each aspect its own omen. A
machine-spirit has two pages more. Its auspex: the scan, the words that print
its omen, and a trial for each omen it declares, whose death-mark 0 tells that
the subject shows it — a trial left empty is none, and the omen without a
trial remains when every trial fails — or the trial of every other omen, under
"*"; the patience of the auspex; and, while the subject is exalted, whether
the auspex is exalted too, spoken by the exalted servitor at invocation alone.
Leaving the scan and every trial empty leaves the subject unread, set at every
invocation. Then its enact words, a row for each omen, where an omen may be
set by the words of every other omen, under "*"; {{omen}} and $SERVITOR_OMEN
name the omen being set. An exalted machine-spirit names its relics upon its
essence, its tongue and the patience of its enact words under its further
rites; it lays down its relics and the exaltation of its auspex once it is no
longer exalted, for they are borne by an exalted subject alone. While the rite
bears observances, the last page of every subject chooses, for each
observance, whether the subject names it as an introit, spoken once before the
first changing subject that names it, as a recessional, spoken once after the
last, or not at all.

At the Observances, a adds an observance once its designation is chosen —
incantation or litany — enter amends it, d strikes it and every naming of it
among the subjects, and J and K move it; a renamed observance is named anew
wherever a subject named it, and tab passes on to the Seal, with or without
observances. Each observance is written on pages: its essence — its name,
whether it is exalted and, exalted, its relics — its further rites, the tongue
and patience, which also unveil its reversion and offerings; its words, or its
scroll and offerings, and its reversion, each written once for every aspect;
and last its guards, a toggle for every aspect it is spoken into, and for
every aspect it is spoken out of: none turned, it is spoken whatever the
aspect.

An edict passes from the Rite through its Liturgy to the Seal. At the Liturgy,
a adds a verse once its kind is chosen, incantation or litany; enter amends
the chosen verse, d casts it out, and J and K move it down and up. Each verse
is written on pages: its essence, then its further rites — patience, tongue,
reversion and offerings, unveiled only when needed — and its words, or its
scroll and offerings, and its reversion, each spoken once. Upon its essence a
verse may be declared exalted, performed with the machine's own authority from
the sanction of its master, and an exalted verse names its relics there, one
per line, laying them down once it is no longer exalted, for relics are borne
by exalted speech alone.

At the Seal the rite stands as the scripture it will become, judged by the
Inquisition: heresy forbids the sealing, and the comments of an amended
scripture are foretold to be lost, for the servitor writes it anew. Once
sealed, the scripture is read back and must yield the very rite the wizard
holds. An edict amended or replicated stays an edict, sealed with "edict":
true and never with aspects; a rite declared an edict, or an edict declared a
rite of aspects, is sealed only once the Inquisition finds no heresy in what
it has become. The Seal judges exaltation too — an exalted vessel that does
not begin at the root, {{former}} within exalted words, a relic outside the
rite's hall or nowhere to be found beside it — and a rite amended or
replicated keeps everything it holds exalted, and its relics.

The key s opens the sanction of the chosen rite, for the cogitator attends as
a terminal does and its master beholds what they sanction. It shows what the
rite holds exalted as it would be sanctioned, every aspect beneath its own
sigil and the whole beneath one, or, when a sanction stands, only what changed
since — the lines lost marked "-", those gained "+" — beneath the sigil it
bore; when nothing changed it says so, and asks no proof. enter then asks the
intercessor for the one proof that lays the sanction, and a asks for the one
that abjures the sanction kept under the rite's name, a forsaken one too; esc
retreats with nothing asked. A rite that holds heresy or nothing exalted
offers nothing to sanction, and the screen tells why.

The cogitator performs what is exalted as every invocation does, and never
with its invoker's authority: once the pre-flight is passed and something
exalted is to change, or a subject only the root may read is to be read, one
exalted servitor is raised through the intercessor with one proof of the
master, and it alone performs each exalted subject, observance and verse in
its turn among the common ones. When the
intercessor is sudo, the cogitator lends it the terminal while it asks for
the master's proof, and takes it back once the exalted servitor first
answers; the words between them never leave their channel. run0 and pkexec
are called upon in a session of their own, where no terminal is theirs to
ask at, so that the graphical agent of the desktop asks; once an act that
called upon run0 has ended, the cogitator speaks run0 -k, so that the next
asks anew. What the intercessor and the exalted servitor speak is kept,
never printed upon the shrine: a refusal before any verse — a sanction that
lapsed or was never laid, no intercessor found, the servitor not installed
as the root's at /usr/bin/servitor, a proof dismissed, an exalted servitor
of another forging or one that refuses — is shown as a heresy with those
words, and nothing is touched; the lament of a fall shows the last words
that exalted speech uttered, and O shows every word, as for any verse.

Know that the cogitator lends no terminal to the words a rite speaks. As in
every invocation, an incantation or litany is given nothing to hear and its
output is captured, never shown as it is spoken; the cogitator holds the
screen and every keystroke for itself. A command that seeks the terminal on
its own — a pager, an editor, a prompt for a password — would contend with
the cogitator for screen and keys, and is struck down when its patience runs
out. Interactive commands are not supported within the cogitator: invoke such
rites from a terminal, or write their commands so that they ask nothing.

Kindred passages: invoke, inquisition, expound, standing, librarium, chronicle,
detached, exalted, sanction, abjure, intercessor
