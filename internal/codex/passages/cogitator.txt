Awaken the cogitator, the interactive shrine of rites

The cogitator is the servitor's shrine for those who would rather behold than
type. It is awakened by the ritual servitor cogitator, or by the servitor
alone when it stands at a terminal; without a terminal the servitor recites
the index of the codex instead.

On its left the cogitator lists every rite of the Librarium with the glyph
of its standing and the aspect it stands in — an edict, which stands in none,
with its standing alone, beside the mark ❢ of an edict; on its right it
reveals the chosen rite: its purpose, its aspects with the current one marked,
its standing, its last rite, its inscriptions, its liturgy with the omen of
every verse, its auspex and where its scripture is recorded. An edict shows
its nature in place of aspects, and its liturgy without omens, for it reveals
none and knows no auspex. Heretical rites are shown too, with the heresy that
taints them.

Roused as root, the servitor awakens no cogitator, for the cogitator performs,
and the servitor performs nothing with the machine's own authority: it refuses
with the death-mark 2. The census, the augury and the Inquisition still read.

From the shrine the faithful choose a rite (j and k, or the arrows; g and G
for the first and last), invoke it with enter, cycle it to its next aspect
with space, consecrate a new rite with n, amend one with e, replicate one
with c, excommunicate one with d, open its scripture in $EDITOR with o,
summon the Inquisition with i, hear the words of the last invocation with O,
read the chronicle with h, filter the rites with /, re-read the Librarium
with r, and retreat with q. The key ? opens the codex itself: above it stands
the cogitator's own catalogue of keys, below it the index of every passage;
enter recites the chosen passage, as servitor expound would.

An edict is never proclaimed on a single key, for it may cast the machine
down. Upon an edict, enter and space alike open its invoke screen, which
offers no aspect to choose: it asks first for the edict's inscriptions, when
it bears any, each written beforehand with its one plain decree, and then
asks whether the edict shall be proclaimed. A second enter proclaims it,
performing its whole liturgy anew; p foresees it, and esc retreats with
nothing performed. While it is proclaimed, its panel, the tidings of its
triumph and the lament of its fall name the edict alone, never a former
aspect and an aspect invoked.

The key h unrolls the chronicle across the whole screen: every invocation of
the current chronicle and of the one rotated before it, newest first, a line
each with its moment, its rite, the former aspect and the aspect invoked — or,
for an edict, the mark ❢ edict in their place — and its verdict. It opens
already filtered to the rite chosen in the list; / rewrites the filter, which
seeks the rite, either aspect and the verdict, or the word edict for every
proclaiming of an edict, and esc while writing casts the filter away so that
every entry is shown. enter reads the chosen entry whole: its inscriptions,
the verse it fell at and the heresy that felled it, and the verdict upon every
reversion. esc returns to the chronicle, and from there to the rites. The
cogitator reads the chronicle that the rune --chronicle, the environment or
the settings place, as every ritual does; it never amends or strikes an entry.

The consecration wizard, awakened by n, e and c, leads a rite through three
stations. At the Rite the faithful speak its name and purpose, choose its
nature — a rite of aspects, or an edict — and speak its aspects, the
inscriptions it bears — each with its purpose, whether it is mandatory, and
its decrees per aspect — and the auspex that reads it. An edict is asked no
aspects and no auspex, and each of its inscriptions takes one plain decree. At
the Liturgy, a adds a step once its kind is chosen among all six, or among
incantation, litany and vox-cast alone for an edict; enter amends the chosen
step, d casts it out, and J and K move it down and up. Each step is written on
pages: first its essence, then one page per aspect for the values that may
differ between aspects, where the choice "same for every aspect" writes a
value once, under "*", for every aspect that speaks none of its own. An edict
speaks the command or scroll of a step, its offerings and its reversion once,
on a single page, and is offered no such choice. Patience, seal, tongue, zeal,
reversion and offerings rest under the further rites, unveiled only when
needed. Upon its essence, every step but a vox-cast may be declared an
exalted verse, performed with the machine's own authority from the sanction
of its master; an exalted incantation or litany names its relics there, one
per line, and lays them down once it is no longer exalted, for relics are
borne by exalted speech alone. At the Seal the rite stands as the scripture it will become, judged by
the Inquisition: heresy forbids the sealing, and the comments of an amended
scripture are foretold to be lost, for the servitor writes it anew. Once
sealed, the scripture is read back and must yield the very rite the wizard
holds. An edict amended or replicated stays an edict, sealed with "edict":
true and never with aspects; a rite declared an edict, or an edict declared a
rite of aspects, is sealed only once the Inquisition finds no heresy in what
it has become — a sanctum, transcription or tether it still holds must first
be cast out. The Seal judges exaltation too — an exalted vessel that does
not begin at the root, {{former}} within exalted words, a relic outside the
rite's hall or nowhere to be found beside it — and a rite amended or
replicated keeps every exalted verse and its relics. The wizard consecrates
exalted verses, yet the cogitator performs none with its invoker's
authority, and lends no intercessor to raise the exalted servitor yet: the
invocation of a rite holding exalted verses, even sanctioned, is refused
before anything is touched — invoke it at a terminal, where one proof of
the master raises the exalted servitor.

Know that the cogitator lends no terminal to the words a rite speaks. As in
every invocation, an incantation or litany is given nothing to hear and its
output is captured, never shown as it is spoken; the cogitator holds the
screen and every keystroke for itself. A command that seeks the terminal on
its own — a pager, an editor, a prompt for a password — would contend with
the cogitator for screen and keys, and is struck down when its patience runs
out. Interactive commands are not supported within the cogitator: invoke such
rites from a terminal, or write their commands so that they ask nothing.

Kindred passages: invoke, inquisition, expound, standing, librarium, chronicle,
detached

Exempla:
  servitor
  servitor cogitator --librarium ~/dotfiles/servitor
  servitor cogitator --chronicle ~/chronicles/theme.jsonl
