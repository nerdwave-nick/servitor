The {{…}} marks the servitor fills in a rite's words at invocation

Hear how one scripture serves many aspects: wherever a rite's words would
differ only by the aspect, the rite, or an inscription, the scribe sets a
placeholder, and at every invocation the servitor illuminates it — fills the
mark with what the invocation knows, before a single step is performed.

These marks are known in every word of a rite:
  {{aspect}}              the aspect being invoked
  {{former}}              the aspect the rite stood in before; empty when
                          the servitor cannot know it
  {{rite.name}}           the rite's own name
  {{inscription.<key>}}   the inscription of that key, from its rune or its
                          decree; empty when neither gives it

An edict bears no aspect, so in its words {{aspect}} and {{former}} are
heresy; {{rite.name}} and {{inscription.<key>}} serve it as they serve every
rite.

The tidings a vox-cast sends are drawn from the servitor's own embedded
lists of tidings, and those alone speak the further marks of the invocation:
{{heresy}} (why it fell), {{step}} (the verse just performed), {{steps}}
(how many true steps the liturgy holds), {{step.kind}} (that step's own key,
such as "tether") and {{step.target}} (what that key holds); those of a rite
of pattern Mark II speak {{subject}} (the subject set last, or the one that
fell) in place of the step's key and target. No word a scribe writes into a
rite may speak them; there they are heresy.

Placeholders are illuminated in the vessels of sanctums and transcriptions,
the names and anchors of tethers, the words of incantations, the scrolls and
offerings of litanies, every reversion, inline scripture, the paths of tomes
and the scripture of tomes that are illuminated, and the auspex. They are
never illuminated in wards, glyphs, tongues, seals, patience, decrees or
purposes, nor in the names of aspects, inscriptions and keys.

A mark the codex does not know, an inscription the rite never declared, and
a mark opened with {{ but never closed are heresy: the inquisition and every
pre-flight denounce them, and they are never rendered empty in silence. A
mark admits no spaces within its braces. To inscribe a literal {{, write
{{{{; a lone }} is taken as written.

Incantations, litanies and the auspex also receive the same knowledge through
the environment: SERVITOR_ASPECT, SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT, SERVITOR_RITE, and
for every declared inscription SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY> — the key in
capitals, each "-" and "." made "_" (empty when unset). An edict's
incantations and litanies receive no SERVITOR_ASPECT and no
SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT. Prefer these over placeholders within the words a
tongue will speak: a placeholder pastes its value into the words before the
tongue reads them, so a quote, a "$" or a ";" within an inscription would be
interpreted by the tongue as part of the command. A variable of the
environment is read by the tongue as one word, untouched, when written within
double quotes.

The words of an exalted verse are fixed when its master sanctions them, and
only marks whose value is known then may be illuminated in them: {{aspect}},
{{rite.name}}, and {{inscription.<key>}}, whose word is then its decree alone
— its rune is refused for that rite, and a mandatory inscription that some
aspect does not decree may not be illuminated there. {{former}} is heresy
there; exalted speech hears the former aspect and the words of runes from
"$SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT" and "$SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY>" instead.

Kindred passages: aspect-maps, inscriptions, illuminate, incantation, vox-cast,
edict, exalted
