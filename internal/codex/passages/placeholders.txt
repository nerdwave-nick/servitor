The {{…}} marks the servitor fills in a rite's words at invocation

Hear how one scripture serves many aspects: wherever a rite's words would
differ only by the aspect, the rite, an inscription or the omen, the scribe
sets a placeholder, and at every invocation the servitor illuminates it —
fills the mark with what the invocation knows, before a single subject is
set.

These marks are known in every word of a rite:
  {{aspect}}              the aspect being invoked; within an omen, the
                          aspect whose omen it is
  {{former}}              the aspect the rite stood in before; empty when
                          the servitor cannot know it
  {{rite.name}}           the rite's own name
  {{inscription.<key>}}   the inscription of that key, from its rune or its
                          decree; empty when neither gives it

And one more, in two places alone:
  {{omen}}                the omen a machine-spirit is enacted into, within
                          its "enact" words, or tried for, within a trial of
                          its auspex; anywhere else it is heresy

An edict bears no aspect, so in its words {{aspect}} and {{former}} are
heresy; {{rite.name}} and {{inscription.<key>}} serve it as they serve every
rite.

The tidings a vox-cast sends are drawn from the servitor's own embedded
lists of tidings, and those alone speak the further marks of the invocation:
{{heresy}} (why it fell), {{subject}} (the subject set last, or the one that
fell), {{step}} and {{steps}} (the count a progress tells, "x / n"), and,
for an edict and for a fallen observance, {{step.kind}} (its designation)
and {{step.target}} (its words or scroll). No word a scribe writes into a rite may speak them; there
they are heresy.

Placeholders are illuminated in the vessels of sanctums and transcriptions,
the bound names of tethers, every omen, the enact words and the scan and
trials of a machine-spirit, the words and scrolls and offerings of
observances and verses, every reversion, the paths of tomes and the
scripture of tomes that are illuminated. An omen is rendered for its own
aspect, with the data-slate's inscriptions when it is read and with the
invocation's when it is set. Placeholders are never illuminated in names,
designations, wards, glyphs, tongues, seals, patience, decrees or purposes,
nor in the names of aspects, inscriptions and keys.

A mark the codex does not know, an inscription the rite never declared, and
a mark opened with {{ but never closed are heresy: the inquisition and every
pre-flight denounce them, and they are never rendered empty in silence. A
mark admits no spaces within its braces. To inscribe a literal {{, write
{{{{; a lone }} is taken as written.

Every word the servitor speaks also receives the same knowledge through the
environment: SERVITOR_ASPECT, SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT, SERVITOR_RITE, for
every declared inscription SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY> — the key in capitals,
each "-" and "." made "_" (empty when unset) — and, for enact words and
trials alone, SERVITOR_OMEN. An edict's words receive no SERVITOR_ASPECT and
no SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT. Prefer these over placeholders within the words
a tongue will speak: a placeholder pastes its value into the words before
the tongue reads them, so a quote, a "$" or a ";" within an inscription —
or within an omen that holds one — would be interpreted by the tongue as
part of the command. A variable of the environment is read by the tongue as
one word, untouched, when written within double quotes.

The words of an exalted subject, observance or verse are fixed when its
master sanctions them, and only marks whose value is known then may be
illuminated in them: {{aspect}}, {{rite.name}}, {{omen}} — every declared
omen is fixed at the sanction — and {{inscription.<key>}}, whose word is then
its decree alone: its rune is refused for that rite, and a mandatory
inscription that some aspect does not decree may not be illuminated there.
An omen of an exalted subject that splices an inscription is held to the
same rule. {{former}} is heresy there; exalted speech hears the former
aspect and the words of runes from "$SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT" and
"$SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY>" instead.

Kindred passages: aspect-maps, omens, enact, trials, inscriptions,
illuminate, vox-cast, edict, exalted
