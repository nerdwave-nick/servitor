A named liturgy that brings the machine into one of its aspects

A rite is the servitor's unit of devotion: one scripture-file in the rites/
hall of the Librarium, written in JSONC — JSON that suffers comments and
trailing commas — and bearing "pattern": "Mark I". The rite is named after
its scripture-file without the extension: rites/theme.json (or theme.jsonc)
is the rite "theme". Only the rites/ hall is read.

A rite's name must begin with a letter or digit and continue only with
letters, digits, ".", "_" or "-". Two scriptures for one name — theme.json
beside theme.jsonc — is heresy, and neither is believed. Every key of the
scripture is examined, and a key the codex does not know is heresy, so a
misspelt key never passes in silence.

The keys of a rite are "pattern", "purpose", "aspects", "inscriptions",
"auspex", "tongue" and "liturgy"; only the pattern and the aspects are
demanded. A heretical rite is shown in the census and the cogitator with its
heresies, but it cannot be invoked until it is purified.

Know also that "rite" is a key of its own within the long form of the
auspex, where it holds the words the auspex speaks:
{"auspex": {"rite": "<words>", "patience": "5s"}}.

Each rite becomes a ritual of its own beneath invoke, with its aspects for
completion and its inscriptions as runes; {{rite.name}} illuminates as its
name, and its commands receive it as SERVITOR_RITE.

Kindred passages: pattern, aspects, liturgy, inscriptions, auspex, librarium

Exempla:
  // <Librarium>/rites/mouse-autohide-toggle.json
  {
    "pattern": "Mark I",
    "purpose": "Hide the mouse cursor after inactivity",
    "aspects": ["on", "off"],
    "liturgy": [
      {"sanctum": "~/.config/niri/util.kdl",
       "scripture": {"on": "cursor {\n    hide-after-inactive-ms 400\n}", "off": ""}},
      {"incantation": "niri msg action do-screen-transition"},
    ],
  }

  servitor invoke mouse-autohide-toggle on
