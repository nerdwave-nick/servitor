The key that names what each line of a vigil tells

Every line the census speaks while it keeps vigil (--vigil) is one binharic
object, and its key "herald" names what that line tells, so that a lesser
machine knows each line by that key alone before it reads further:

  "rite"       a rite, whole: every key of its entry in the binharic
               census — rite, edict, aspect, standing, auspex, aspects,
               purpose, desecrated, last_rite, recorded_in and canon —
               set flat beside the herald. A later "rite" line of the same
               rite replaces the earlier one entirely
  "lament"     a lament spoken in the course of the vigil, such as a
               data-slate that cannot be read: its words under "lament",
               and under "rite" the rite it concerns, whenever it is known
  "concluded"  the vigil's last line, told exactly once, when every rite
               is settled; it bears nothing but its herald

A rite told before its auspex answered bears "auspex": "awaited", and is
told once more when it settles. Keys are only ever added, never taken away,
and further heralds may be proclaimed in time to come: a machine that reads
the vigil passes over a herald it does not know rather than falling. The
census spoken without --vigil knows no herald.

A lesser machine forged in Rust reads every line into one enumeration
tagged by the herald, with a fallback for heralds yet unknown:

  #[serde(tag = "herald", rename_all = "snake_case")]

Kindred passages: vigil, lament, census, auspex, binharic, canon

Exempla:
  {"herald":"rite","rite":"theme","edict":false,"aspect":"","standing":"dormant","auspex":"awaited",...}
  {"herald":"lament","lament":"the data-slate ... is garbled beyond the servitor's reading; ...","rite":"theme"}
  {"herald":"concluded"}

  servitor census --vigil | jq -r 'select(.herald == "lament") | .lament'
