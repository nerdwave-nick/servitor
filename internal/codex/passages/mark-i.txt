The hall where scripture of pattern Mark I is kept once converted

When the servitor converts a scripture of pattern "Mark I" to "Mark II", it
first keeps the scripture as it was read, byte for byte and remarks and all,
in a hall named .mark-i beside it:

  <Librarium>/rites/.mark-i/<name>.json     a rite (or .jsonc, as written)
  <Librarium>/.mark-i/servitor.json         the settings

The hall is raised when the first copy is kept. A copy is opened so that
one already standing is never written over: should a copy stand that
differs from the scripture, the conversion is refused, lamented, and the
scripture stays of pattern Mark I until that copy is moved aside; it is
then converted at its next reading.
A copy that is the scripture itself, kept by a conversion felled before it
could write, lets the conversion proceed.

The Librarium never reads .mark-i: it passes over halls and every name
that begins with ".", so a copy is never a rite, never counted by the
census and never judged by the Inquisition. The servitor never strikes a
copy either; strike it yourself once the converted rite has served you
well.

To return to the elder scripture, the faithful have but one road: the
servitor reads no Mark I without converting it, and converts nothing back.
Read the copy to learn what a rite said before, and write what it should
now say in pattern Mark II; the remarks of the converted scripture name
whatever was struck or changed in meaning. Should the conversion itself be
wanted anew, move the copy back into the place of the converted scripture:
the next ritual that may write converts it again, and keeps its copy anew.

Kindred passages: conversion, pattern, librarium, rite

Exempla:
  ls ~/.config/servitor/rites/.mark-i/
  diff ~/.config/servitor/rites/.mark-i/dnd.json ~/.config/servitor/rites/dnd.json

  mv ~/.config/servitor/rites/.mark-i/dnd.json ~/.config/servitor/rites/dnd.json
  servitor census    # converts dnd anew, and keeps its copy again
