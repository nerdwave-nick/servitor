Ask whether a rite stands in one aspect, answered by exit alone

The rune --is <aspect> is given to the ritual augury. Instead of speaking
what it reads, the augury then answers a single question by its exit alone:
0 when the rite stands in that aspect, 1 when it does not — whether it stands
in another aspect, lies dormant, or its omens disagree. Nothing is spoken
upon a match or a miss, so the rune serves the conditions of other commands
and rites.

The aspect named must be one the rite declares, and the augury exits 2 when it
is not, when the rite is unknown or heretical, or when a key is given beside
the rune: --is asks after the aspect alone. An edict stands in no aspect, so
--is upon an edict is refused with a heresy and exit 2; ask after its standing
instead. The auspex is awakened, as in every augury, so the answer heeds the
true aspect of the machine.

The aspects of the rite are offered by completion after --is; for an edict
nothing is offered.

Kindred passages: augury, aspects, omens, standing, auspex, edict

Exempla:
  servitor augury mouse-autohide-toggle --is on && echo "the cursor is veiled"
  if servitor augury dnd --is on; then makoctl mode -r do-not-disturb; fi
  [ "$(servitor augury screenshot standing)" = proclaimed ]
