The death-marks the servitor itself bears when an invocation ends

As an incantation ends bearing a death-mark, so does the servitor. The
ritual invoke tells by its death-mark alone how the invocation ended, so
that lesser machines may branch upon it without reading a word, and it is
the same whether or not the rune --binharic was spoken:

  0  triumph: every step was performed, the edict obeyed
  1  reverted: a step fell, and every deed was undone; the machine stands
     as it stood before
  2  refused before anything was touched: the pre-flight found heresy, the
     rite or the aspect is unknown, words strayed after an edict's name, a
     rune was malformed, or --foresee was spoken with --binharic; for a
     rite with exalted verses also a sanction lapsed or never laid, no
     intercessor or no servitor installed as it must be, or the master's
     proof dismissed
  3  faltered: a step fell, and a reversion fell too; the rite lies
     corrupted until it is invoked anew

A servitor roused as root refuses every invocation with 2, for it performs
nothing with the machine's own authority.

The death-mark 2 is the augury's own mark for what it could not read. An
invocation halted by its master's interrupt is reverted like any other that
fell, and bears 1, or 3 should a reversion falter. A foresight bears 0 when
its pre-flight was pure and 2 when it was not.

Only the forbiddance of the pre-flight and the verdicts speak upon stdout
in binharic; every other refusal bearing 2 speaks upon stderr alone. The
augury and the Inquisition keep marks of their own, recited in their
passages.

Kindred passages: verdicts, invoke, binharic, forbidden, augury,
inquisition

Exempla:
  servitor invoke theme porpl; echo "death-mark $?"

  servitor invoke mouse-autohide-toggle on -s
  case $? in
    0) echo "the Omnissiah is pleased" ;;
    1) echo "reverted; the machine stands as before" ;;
    2) echo "forbidden; nothing was touched" ;;
    3) echo "faltered; summon the Inquisition" ;;
  esac
