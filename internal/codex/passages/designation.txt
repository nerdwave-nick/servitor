The key that tells which shape a subject, observance or entry takes

In pattern "Mark II" every object that may take several shapes names its
shape under "designation", and the keys it may hold follow from it alone.
A subject is designated:

  "sanctum"         a warded region of a vessel: "vessel", "ward", "glyph",
                    "closing-glyph", "consecrate"; its omens are scripture
  "transcription"   a vessel written whole: "vessel", "seal", "zeal"; its
                    omens are scripture, or null where the vessel shall not be
  "tether"          a bound name: "tether", "zeal"; its omens are anchors, or
                    null where the name shall be unbound
  "machine-spirit"  read by its "auspex", set by its "enact" words, spoken in
                    its "tongue" with its "patience" and "relics"

An observance, and an entry of an edict's liturgy, is designated
"incantation", speaking its "words", or "litany", reciting its "scroll" with
its "offerings".

A designation missing, or one the codex does not know for its place, is
heresy; so is a key that belongs to another designation. A sanctum,
transcription or tether reads and sets itself by its vessel or its link, and
bears no auspex, enact words, tongue, patience or relics of its own.

Kindred passages: subjects, observances, machine-spirit, sanctum,
transcription, tether, incantation, litany
