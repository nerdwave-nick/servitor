What a rite of pattern Mark II reads and sets, each by itself

A rite of pattern "Mark II" keeps no liturgy. Under "subjects" it declares,
in written order, every thing of the machine it brings into its aspects: a
sanctum in a vessel, a vessel transcribed whole, a tethered name, or a
machine-spirit read by its auspex and set by its enact words. Each subject
is named once under "name", takes its shape from its "designation", and
declares under "omens" the mark each aspect leaves upon it.

At invocation every subject is read; those already bearing the omen of the
aspect invoked are passed over, and only those whose omen differs are set,
each proven by a second reading, and restored to what was read should the
invocation fall. A subject may name observances to be spoken before it
changes ("introit") or after ("recessional").

The list must hold at least one subject: a rite of aspects with nothing to
read or set can bring the machine into no aspect, and is heresy. An edict
bears no subjects. A subject may be exalted, set with the machine's own
authority from a sanction; its words are then fixed at that sanction.

An exalted subject is set by the exalted servitor alone, and only when it
is to change (see exalted). Its auspex may itself be marked "exalted", for
what only the root can read: such an auspex is spoken by the exalted
servitor at invocation alone, which tells only the declared omens it
fits, and never by an augury, a census, a completion or a foresight, which
tell the omen last recorded, stale.

Kindred passages: designation, omens, machine-spirit, observances, introit,
recessional, pattern, exalted, sanction
