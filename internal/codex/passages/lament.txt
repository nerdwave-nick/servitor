The words of a lament, as a vigil tells them

Where the servitor cannot read what it needs, it laments. A census spoken
whole speaks its laments upon stderr, apart from the census itself, and
still counts the rite: a data-slate that cannot be read is lamented, and
the rite is read as if it had none. A sanction that cannot be read is
lamented too, and its rite has lapsed; a forsaken sanction, whose rite is
gone or holds no exalted verse, is lamented after every rite, and in a
vigil before the first.

While the census keeps vigil (--vigil), stdout bears binharic alone, so a
lament is told there instead, as a line of its own under the herald
"lament":

  herald   "lament"
  lament   the words of the lament, as the servitor would speak them
  rite     the rite the lament concerns; absent when no rite is known

A lament may come anywhere before the conclusion of the vigil. It changes
nothing of the "rite" lines: the rite it names is told as ever, read as if
it kept no data-slate. Further laments may be told in time to come,
named by the same herald.

Kindred passages: herald, vigil, census, data-slate, sanction

Exempla:
  {"herald":"lament","lament":"the data-slate ... resists every attempt to read it","rite":"theme"}

  servitor census --vigil | jq -r 'select(.herald == "lament") | "\(.rite): \(.lament)"'
