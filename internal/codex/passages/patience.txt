How long a command may labour before it is deemed to have fallen

Patience is the span of time the servitor grants a command before it loses
faith in it. It is written as a span the machine understands: a number with
its measure, such as "500ms", "5s", "2m" or "1h30m"; it must be greater than
nothing.

The enact words of a machine-spirit, an observance and a verse of an edict
labour under their own patience, else the patience of the settings, else
30s; a reversion is granted the same. When the patience runs out, the
servitor slays the command together with every process it summoned, and it
falls — whereupon the invocation is reverted. An auspex has a patience of
its own, written within it, {"scan": …, "patience": …}, and 2s when none is
written; it bounds the scan and every trial together, and an auspex that
runs out of patience is mute and gives no omen.

Patience written in the settings serves every enact word, observance and
verse of the Librarium that names none; it never sways an auspex. A patience the
servitor cannot read is heresy. The patience of exalted words, wherever it
was written, is part of their sanction: changing it, upon them or in the
settings, lapses the sanction; so does the patience of an auspex itself
marked exalted.

Kindred passages: incantation, litany, auspex, reversion, tongue, sanction

Exempla:
  // on an observance
  {"name": "rebuild", "designation": "litany",
   "scroll": "scripts/rebuild-cache", "patience": "2m"}

  // on an auspex
  "auspex": {"scan": "test -e /run/user/1000/veiled && echo on || echo off",
             "patience": "500ms"}

  // in servitor.json
  {"pattern": "Mark II", "patience": "10s"}
