How long a command may labour before it is deemed to have fallen

Patience is the span of time the servitor grants a command before it loses
faith in it. It is written as a span the machine understands: a number with
its measure, such as "500ms", "5s", "2m" or "1h30m"; it must be greater than
nothing.

An incantation or litany labours under the patience of its step, else the
patience of the settings, else 30s; its reversion is granted the same. When
the patience runs out, the servitor slays the command together with every
process it summoned, and the step falls — whereupon the invocation is
reverted. An auspex has a patience of its own, written in its long form
{"rite": …, "patience": …}, and 2s when none is written; an auspex that runs
out of patience simply gives no omen.

Patience written in the settings serves every incantation and litany of the
Librarium that names none; it never sways the auspex. A patience the
servitor cannot read is heresy.

Kindred passages: incantation, litany, auspex, reversion, tongue

Exempla:
  // on a step
  {"litany": "scripts/rebuild-cache", "patience": "2m"}

  // on an auspex
  "auspex": {"rite": "test -e /run/user/1000/veiled && echo on || echo off",
             "patience": "500ms"}

  // in servitor.json
  {"pattern": "Mark I", "patience": "10s"}
