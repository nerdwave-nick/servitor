The record of every invocation, and the rune and key that place it

The chronicle is the servitor's memory of its deeds. Every invocation that was
performed — whether it ended in triumph, was reverted, or faltered, and
whether it ran its course or was halted from the terminal — adds one line of
binharic to the chronicle: when it happened, the rite, the aspect invoked and
the former aspect, the inscriptions it carried, its verdict, and "changed",
the subjects it set and proved, in order — [] when it changed none. For a
fallen invocation it names what fell under "fell_at" — {"subject": …} or
{"observance": …} — the heresy that felled it, and the verdict of every
reversion, each named by its subject or observance. The line of an edict
names no aspect, no former aspect and nothing changed; it bears "edict":
true in their stead, and names a fallen verse by its place, "verse", and
its designation and words, and each reversion by its place. The line of an invocation in which the exalted servitor was
raised names, last, the intercessor that vouched for its master
("intercessor") and the first twelve letters of the sigil of the plan the
exalted servitor performed ("sigil"); an invocation that changed nothing
exalted raised none, and names neither. A proof dismissed performed nothing,
and is not chronicled. An invocation the pre-flight forbade, or one merely
foreseen, performed nothing and is not chronicled.

The chronicle is kept at the first of these that speaks: the universal rune
--chronicle, given to any ritual; then the environment's SERVITOR_CHRONICLE;
then the key "chronicle" of the settings; then
$XDG_STATE_HOME/servitor/chronicle.jsonl (or ~/.local/state/servitor/ when
$XDG_STATE_HOME is not set). A leading "~" and the $NAMES of the environment
are expanded; a relative path given by rune or environment is reckoned from
the hall the servitor was summoned in, and one given in the settings from
the Librarium.

The chronicle is not suffered to grow without end: when a line would carry
it past one mebibyte, the chronicle is renamed with the suffix .1, replacing
the one so named before, and a fresh chronicle begins. Thus the current and
the previous chronicle endure, and older memory is forgotten. Halls missing
above the chronicle are raised when the first line is written.

In the cogitator the key h unrolls the chronicle, current and rotated
alike, newest first and filtered to the chosen rite; enter reads one entry
whole.

Kindred passages: verdicts, verse, invoke, data-slate, librarium,
inscriptions, cogitator
