Engrave the completion litanies of the servitor into your terminal

The completion ritual speaks a litany of completion for the tongue of your
terminal — bash, zsh, fish or powershell — which, once sourced, lets the Tab
key complete the servitor's words as they are typed. The litany is not
static: on every Tab it asks the servitor itself, so it always knows the
rites of the Librarium as they stand now.

It offers the rituals; the rites of the Librarium, each with its purpose;
the aspects of a rite, the aspect it stands in marked as the current aspect;
the inscription runes of a rite, each with its purpose, and the values its
decrees grant, each naming the aspects that decree it; the keys of an
augury; the aspects for --is; the rites for the Inquisition; and every topic
of the codex for expound, each with its summing line. The rune --librarium
completes halls, and --chronicle the names of scripture-files.

Completion must answer at once, so it never awakens an auspex: the current
aspect it marks is read from the omens of the steps and the data-slate alone.

Bash requires the bash-completion package to be installed; zsh requires
compinit to have been called. Each tongue keeps its litanies in its own
place; the exempla below engrave them for the present session or for good.

Kindred passages: invoke, augury, expound, inscriptions, decrees, auspex

Exempla:
  source <(servitor completion bash)
  servitor completion bash \
    > ~/.local/share/bash-completion/completions/servitor

  servitor completion zsh > "${fpath[1]}/_servitor"

  servitor completion fish | source
  servitor completion fish > ~/.config/fish/completions/servitor.fish
