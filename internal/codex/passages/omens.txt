The mark an aspect leaves upon a subject, and how the omens agree

An omen is the mark an aspect leaves upon a subject: the scripture a sanctum
or transcribed vessel holds in that aspect, the anchor a tether leads to, or
the words a machine-spirit shows. Under "omens" each subject of a rite of
pattern Mark II declares the omen of each aspect, as an object keyed by
aspect with "*" serving every aspect not named. An omen shared by several
aspects fits them all. A machine-spirit without "omens" bears the name of
each aspect as its omen. Within an omen, {{aspect}} names the aspect whose
omen it is, so {"*": "~/.config/nfluff/themes/{{aspect}}"} gives every aspect
an anchor of its own; inscriptions spliced into an omen take the
data-slate's words when reading, and the invocation's when setting.

The servitor does not trust its memory alone: to know which aspect a rite
stands in, it reads every subject, and each yields as its omen the set of
aspects it fits:

  sanctum         the aspect its begin marker records
  transcription   every aspect whose rendered scripture its vessel holds;
                  a vessel absent fits every aspect whose omen is null
  tether          every aspect whose rendered anchor the bound name leads
                  to; a name unbound fits every aspect whose omen is null
  machine-spirit  every aspect whose omen its auspex reads (see scan and
                  trials)

A subject that fits no aspect, a mute auspex, a machine-spirit without an
auspex, and one whose auspex is exalted — never spoken by the augury —
yield no omen of their own.

The omens agree by intersection: each narrows the aspects the machine may
stand in, and the aspects every omen fits are the rite's "fitting". When
they fit none, the rite is corrupted, though each omen be shared by two
aspects; one names its aspect; several name the aspect of the data-slate
when it is among them, else none, and the rite is desecrated when the
data-slate recorded another, dormant when it recorded none. When no subject
yields an omen, the data-slate is heeded. The passage on the standing tells
all.

Every invocation of a rite of aspects records on the data-slate the omen
each machine-spirit was known to show at its end. Where the live omen of a
subject is not heard — its auspex is exalted, spared by --spare-auspex or
by completion, or still labouring in the first telling of a vigil or the
cogitator — that recorded omen serves in its stead, marked "stale": true
with "read_at", the hour it was read, and fits aspects as a live one does.
It is ignored when it was recorded in another boot of the machine, or when
it is no longer a declared omen of the subject. A stale omen never
desecrates a rite on its own: it was recorded together with the data-slate's
aspect, so only a live omen bears witness against that aspect. An
invocation never trusts a recorded omen: it reads every subject live.

The augury lists every omen in written order, named by its subject and
designation, with "omen" — the words read, when a machine-spirit showed one
declared omen — "aspects", every aspect it fits, and "aspect" when it fits
but one. The litanies of completion never awaken an auspex; neither does a
foresight speak an auspex marked exalted. An edict yields no omen at all:
it reads and sets nothing, and its standing is judged from its data-slate
alone.

Kindred passages: standing, auspex, scan, trials, augury, data-slate,
subjects, machine-spirit, spare-auspex, vigil
