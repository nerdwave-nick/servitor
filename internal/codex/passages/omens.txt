What each step of a rite reveals of the aspect the machine stands in

The servitor does not trust its memory alone. To know which aspect a rite
stands in, it reads the machine itself, and every step that can be observed
yields an omen — the aspect that step bears witness to.

  sanctum         the aspect recorded in its begin marker
  tether          the aspect whose anchor the bound name leads to; a name
                  unbound bears witness to an aspect whose anchor is null
  transcription   the aspect whose scripture its vessel holds; a vessel
                  absent bears witness to an aspect whose scripture is null
  auspex          the aspect the rite's own scanning command proclaims

Incantations, litanies and vox-casts leave nothing behind to be read, and so
give no omen; neither does a vessel that cannot be read, nor a step whose
observation fits several aspects at once. The auspex gives none when it
exceeds its patience, when it ends bearing a death-mark, or when it speaks
anything but a declared aspect. An edict yields no omen at all: it holds only
incantations, litanies and vox-casts and bears no auspex, and its standing
is judged from its data-slate alone.

To compare, the servitor renders each observed field for every aspect, with
the inscriptions its data-slate recorded for that aspect, or else that
aspect's decrees; so inscriptions may dwell in an anchor and still be read.

From the omens the standing is judged: all agreeing name the aspect, any
disagreement corrupts the rite, and where no omen speaks the data-slate is
heeded. The augury lists every omen in written order, the auspex last, each
named by its verse and its own key. The litanies of completion never awaken
the auspex, and neither does foreseeing.

A rite of pattern Mark II reads its subjects instead: a sanctum yields the
aspect its marker records, a transcription and a tether every aspect whose
omen they match, a machine-spirit every aspect whose omen its auspex reads
(the passages on scan and trials tell all). An omen shared by several
aspects fits them all. A subject that fits none, a mute auspex, a
machine-spirit without an auspex and one whose auspex is exalted — never
spoken by the augury — yield no omen. The omens agree by intersection: the
aspects every omen fits are the rite's "fitting". None corrupts the rite;
one names its aspect; several name the aspect of the data-slate when it is
among them, else none, and the rite is desecrated when the data-slate
recorded another, dormant when it recorded none. Where every omen fits one
aspect, this is the agreement told above. Each omen is named by its
subject and designation, with the omen read when a machine-spirit showed
one, every aspect it fits, and its aspect when it fits but one.

Kindred passages: standing, auspex, augury, data-slate, verse, subjects,
scan, trials
