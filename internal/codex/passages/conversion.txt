How scripture of pattern Mark I becomes Mark II, once, on its first reading

The servitor performs rites of pattern "Mark II" alone. Scripture of the
elder pattern "Mark I" is converted: read as Mark I, it is turned into the
rite of Mark II that does what it did, and every ritual then knows the rite
converted. The faithful write nothing by hand.

Who writes the conversion. The first ritual that reads pure scripture of
Mark I and may write converts it on the disk: invoke, augury, census,
inquisition, sanction, abjure and every other ritual of the servitor, before
it is performed. Completion, foresight (--foresee) and a servitor roused as
root never write: they read the rite converted in their memory alone, and
the scripture stays of pattern Mark I until a ritual that may write reads
it. The exalted servitor reads no Librarium, and converts nothing. The
cogitator converts as it reads, and tells it in a toast.

How it is written. The scripture of Mark I is first kept, unchanged, in the
hall .mark-i beside it — rites/.mark-i/<name>.json, or .jsonc — opened so
that a copy already standing there is never written over (see mark-i). Then
the converted scripture takes its place whole and at once, under the
dominion of its rite and with the seal it bore. Should another invocation
hold that dominion, the servitor does not wait: the rite stays converted in
memory, and is written at a later reading. A scripture changed since it was
read, or a copy that stands and differs from it, is not written over; the
servitor laments it, and the scripture stays as it was. A scripture tethered
into rites/ is converted where it lies, so that the tether endures.

The settings are converted alike, yet only their "pattern" is rewritten in
place, so that every remark in them survives; their scripture of Mark I is
kept at <Librarium>/.mark-i/servitor.json. Settings of Mark II keep the
keys of Mark I.

What it tells. Each conversion is told in one line of tidings, upon stderr,
prefixed "⋯": the rite, where its scripture of Mark I is kept, and what
changed in meaning. It is told even under --binharic, which keeps stdout to
its binharic alone, and is withheld by --silence. A rite that held exalted
verses is told that the sanction of its master lapses, for the unit of its
exalted words changes with its pattern, and the tidings name servitor
sanction <rite>.

What becomes what, for a rite of aspects:

  "pattern": "Mark I"        "pattern": "Mark II"
  a sanctum, transcription   a subject of that designation, named
  or tether                  "<kind>-<verse>"; its scripture or anchor
                             becomes its "omens" (one value for every
                             aspect becomes {"*": …}); its other keys are
                             kept, and so are the markers of its vessel,
                             so no vessel is written anew
  an incantation or litany   an observance named "<kind>-<verse>", spoken
  beside them                as the introit of the next subject after it,
                             else as the recessional of the last before
                             it; words that differ by aspect become one
                             observance per word, guarded by "to" and
                             named "<kind>-<verse>-<first aspect>"
  an incantation or litany   a machine-spirit named "<kind>-<verse>",
  in a rite of none of them  without omens, its "enact" keyed by aspect;
                             its reversion is struck, for a machine-spirit
                             is restored by enacting the omen it showed
                             before; a litany is enacted by words that call
                             its scroll with its offerings
  the rite's "auspex"        the "scan" of its only machine-spirit, when it
                             has exactly one; struck otherwise
  vox-cast steps             the rite's "vox-cast" list: "progress" when it
                             held a progress, "success" when a success
  decrees                    kept, always keyed by aspect

An edict keeps its liturgy, each verse taking the designation form, and its
vox-casts become the list. Whatever is struck or changes meaning — the
auspex of the rite, a reversion, a litany spoken as enact words — is kept as
a remark at its new place, quoting the words of Mark I, and named in the
tidings. The remarks of the scripture of Mark I are not carried over: they
endure in its copy. Data-slates and the chronicle need no conversion.

Scripture of Mark I that holds heresy is not converted: one more heresy at
its "pattern" tells that it is converted once its heresies are purged.
Scripture whose conversion would be heretical — a rite of vox-casts alone,
which would read and set nothing — is not converted either, and each heresy
of what it would become is told upon its "pattern". Nothing ever converts
Mark II back to Mark I.

Kindred passages: pattern, mark-i, subjects, observances, machine-spirit,
vox-cast, sanction, librarium, silence

Exempla:
  servitor census
  # upon stderr, once, before the census, a line beginning with ⋯:
  #   the rite "dnd" is converted to pattern "Mark II"; its scripture of
  #   pattern "Mark I" is kept at ~/.config/servitor/rites/.mark-i/dnd.json;
  #   changed in meaning, as the comments of its scripture tell: the auspex
  #   of the rite is struck

  // a step of Mark I …
  {"tether": "~/.local/share/nfluff/current-theme",
   "anchor": "~/.config/nfluff/themes/{{aspect}}"}
  // … becomes a subject of Mark II
  {"name": "tether-2", "designation": "tether",
   "tether": "~/.local/share/nfluff/current-theme",
   "omens": {"*": "~/.config/nfluff/themes/{{aspect}}"}}
