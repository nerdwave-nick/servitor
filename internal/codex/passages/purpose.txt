The words that tell a reader why a rite or an inscription exists

A "purpose" may be written for a rite and for each of its inscriptions. It
is free words for the eyes of the faithful; the servitor never illuminates
it, so placeholders written there are kept exactly as written and are not
denounced. A purpose is never required, yet a rite without one is a mystery
to everyone who meets it later, the scribe included.

The purpose of a rite is spoken wherever the rite is named to a reader: in
the census and its binharic answer (key "purpose"), in the detail panel of
the cogitator, beside the rite's name when completion offers it, and as the
short lore of the rite's own ritual beneath "servitor invoke". A rite
without purpose is offered with its aspects instead.

The purpose of an inscription is spoken beside its rune on the rite's own
page of lore and when completion offers the inscription's key to an augury,
so the faithful know what word to inscribe.

Kindred passages: rite, inscriptions, census, cogitator

  {
    "pattern": "Mark I",
    "purpose": "The visage of the machine",
    "aspects": ["default", "finii", "porpl"],
    "inscriptions": {
      "reason": {"purpose": "why the rite was invoked"}
    },
    "liturgy": [{"incantation": "nfluff apply {{aspect}}"}]
  }
