The ordered verses of an edict, proclaimed one after another as written

The "liturgy" is the edict's alone: a list of verses, each an incantation or
a litany, proclaimed whole and in the order written every time the edict is
invoked. A rite of aspects of pattern Mark II keeps no liturgy — it reads
and sets its subjects, and speaks its observances when they change — and a
liturgy upon it is heresy whose words name its subjects and observances.

Every verse is one object in the designation form: "designation":
"incantation" with its "words", or "designation": "litany" with its
"scroll" and "offerings", each with its "tongue", "patience", "reversion",
"exalted" and "relics" — an observance's keys, without "name", "to" and
"from". A missing or unknown designation, or a key of another designation,
is heresy. Every value is written once and plainly, for an edict bears no
aspect.

At invocation the pre-flight first examines every verse without touching
anything; only then are the verses performed, in the order written, never
in parallel and never reordered. Should one fall, the verses already
performed are reverted in reverse order, the fallen one included, each by
its own reversion words, and the machine returns to how it stood before
the edict was proclaimed. A verse is named by its place, counted from one:
"verse 2 · incantation …". The edict's "vox-cast" list tells of each verse
performed and of the whole obeyed; it is no verse.

Kindred passages: edict, incantation, litany, verse, vox-cast, pre-flight,
reversion, subjects, conversion
