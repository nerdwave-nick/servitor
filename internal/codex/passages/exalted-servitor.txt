The servitor roused with the machine's own authority to perform the exalted verses of one invocation

The exalted servitor is no ritual of its own. It is the servitor installed at
/usr/bin/servitor, roused by the intercessor for one invocation alone, as

  /usr/bin/servitor __exalt <rite> <sigil> [<aspect>]

where the sigil is the first twelve letters of the sigil of the plan it is to
perform, and an edict names no aspect. It bears the machine's own authority,
and refuses to begin without it: roused by hand it says so, touches nothing
and bears the death-mark 2. It is never listed among the rituals, and it
never reads the Librarium, the settings, the data-slates or the chronicle of
the one who invokes it, nor the home that sudo names.

Before it hears a word, it judges the scroll it truly runs from, as the
machine names it in /proc/self/exe: the root's own, written by none but the
root, it and every hall above it, and the very scroll that stands at that
name. A servitor that breaks this rule refuses, touches nothing and bids its
master install the servitor anew.

It hears the invoker's servitor over one channel alone: a socketpair upon its
descriptors 0 and 1, in strict JSON lines of at most 1 MiB, every member
known. The first line names the protocol, the master, the rite, the aspect
and the whole sigil; each must agree with the words that roused it, and the
master with the uid the machine knows at the other end of the channel (or
with SUDO_UID, when sudo lays pipes between them). A servitor forged anew
that speaks another protocol than the one installed is refused: install the
servitor anew, so that both are of one forging. Whether it accepts the
first line or refuses it, its answer names the protocol it speaks, so that a
servitor forged anew knows an installed servitor of another forging, refuses
with the death-mark 2 and bids the same. The words given at
invocation, the former aspect and the runes of inscriptions, travel in that
first line too, at most 4 KiB each, never bearing a NUL or a newline.

It then reads the sanction of the master from the root's store, following no
tether and trusting nothing the root did not lay, and performs only the plan
of that sanction that bears the sigil it was given: never the rite as the
invoker holds it now. Before the first verse it seizes a dominion of its own
beneath /run/servitor/dominions/, over the master's rite and over every
exalted vessel and bound name, waiting as every invocation waits. A dominion
it cannot hold refuses the invocation before anything is touched.

Thereafter it hears four words alone: perform the next exalted verse, revert
the last one performed, finish, abort. A word out of its order, or a second
plan, is refused, and ends the session. Of a vessel it tells only whether it
changed, its size and its SHA-256, never its scripture. Its vessels and
tethers are walked from the root through halls the root alone may write, and
transcribed atomically, keeping their owner, and their seal unless the verse
decrees one, as every transcription does.

Exalted incantations and litanies it speaks itself, in a working hall of its
own: /run/servitor/<sigil>/, raised afresh for the session, the root's and
sealed 0700, holding the relics of the plan under their own names and the
scroll of each litany. Its tongue must begin at the root and be a program
the root owns and alone may write, reached through halls the root alone may
write, and it must be bash, zsh, fish, sh or dash by the last name of the
program it truly is, each bidden to read no scripture of its own as it
awakens: "bash --noprofile --norc", "zsh -f", "fish --no-config", sh and
dash as they are; any other tongue refuses the session before anything is
touched. A scroll whose first line names an interpreter is recited by that
interpreter, judged by the trusted-path rule alike. Speech hears no environment but what the exalted servitor grants:
PATH=/usr/local/sbin:/usr/local/bin:/usr/bin, HOME=/root, LANG and LC_* when
they truly name a locale, SERVITOR_RITE, SERVITOR_ASPECT,
SERVITOR_FORMER_ASPECT (never for an edict) and SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY>.
Each verse is spoken in a session and process group of its own, its input
empty, under its patience; when it runs out, the servitor slays the verse
with every process it summoned. Reversions are spoken by the exalted
servitor too, in the same hall. The last 64 KiB of what speech utters
return to the invoker's servitor, so that the lament of a fallen verse can
show them. When the session ends, however it ends, every process its speech
left behind is slain and the working hall is cast down: a root process meant
to last belongs in a unit of the machine (systemd-run --unit=…).

Should its invoker fall before finish (the channel ends, ten minutes pass
without a word, or SIGTERM reaches it), it reverts every verse it performed,
from the last back to the first, and ends. It dwells at the root of the
machine with umask 022, dumps no core, lets no descriptor pass to what it
summons, and heeds no interrupt, hang-up or halt of the terminal — though
what it summons heeds them as ever. Each of
its acts is kept in the journal of the machine: uid, rite, aspect, verse,
sigil and verdict.

Roused as "__exalt --sanction <rite> <sigil>", where the sigil is the first
twelve letters of the sigil of the whole unit, it keeps the store of
sanctions instead, for the master at the other end of the channel, never
for one argv names. The first line names the protocol, the master, the
rite, the whole sigil and how many bytes the unit holds; the unit follows
in its canonical form, in lines of at most 512 KiB of it each (written in
base64), at most 63 MiB in all. The unit must bear the sigil both name, be
the master's and of the rite argv names, and pass every rule an invocation
holds its plan to: every vessel and bound name from the root and beneath
none of /proc, /sys and /dev, every tongue, shebang, relic and variable of
its speech as exalted speech would judge them, raising nothing. Only then
is it laid, in the store opened as it is read, following no tether. Roused
as "__exalt --abjure <rite>" or "__exalt --abjure --all", it hears one line
naming the protocol, the master and the rite, or every rite, and strikes
those sanctions. Either way it answers once, naming its protocol, and
journals what it laid (with the sigil it replaced, "none" or "unreadable")
or struck; whatever it refuses leaves the store as it stood, its death-mark
2.

Kindred passages: exalted, sanction, abjure, sigil, relics, intercessor,
dominion, tongue, verdicts

Exempla:
  journalctl -t servitor
  … servitor: uid=1000 rite="split-lock" aspect="on" verse=1 sigil=4f0c2a9e1b7d deed=perform verdict=triumph
  … servitor: uid=1000 rite="split-lock" aspect="on" sigil=4f0c2a9e1b7d deed=finish verdict=finished
  … servitor: uid=1000 rite="split-lock" sigil=d66ec48808fd former=none deed=sanction verdict=sanctioned
