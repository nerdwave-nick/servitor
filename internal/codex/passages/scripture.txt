What a sanctum or transcription places into its vessel

Scripture is the holy text a subject sets down: the region a sanctum keeps
between its markers, or the whole of a vessel a transcription writes. It is
the omen of such a subject, written under its "omens", one for each aspect
with "*" serving every aspect not named; the key "scripture" itself is the
elder pattern Mark I's, which conversion makes those omens. Each scripture
may be written in three forms:

  - a string, the scripture itself, newlines as \n;
  - a list of lines, joined one to the next with a newline;
  - a tome, {"tome": "<path>", "illuminate": true}, drawn from another
    scripture-file, for scripture too long or too precious to inline.

An object that holds "tome" or "illuminate" is a tome, never mistaken for a
map of aspects.

Inline scripture is always illuminated: its placeholders — {{aspect}},
{{former}}, {{rite.name}}, {{inscription.<key>}} — are filled at every
invocation, and {{{{ writes a literal {{. An unknown placeholder or an
inscription the rite never declared is heresy, never rendered empty. A tome
is read verbatim unless it is illuminated.

For a transcription, scripture may be null for an aspect, decreeing that the
vessel shall not be. For a sanctum, null is heresy; write "" to keep an empty
sanctum instead.

The servitor also reads scripture to know the aspect: a transcribed vessel's
scripture is compared with the scripture of each aspect, and a sanctum whose
scripture is no longer that of its recorded aspect is tainted.

Kindred passages: sanctum, transcription, omens, tome, illuminate,
aspect-maps, placeholders, conversion

Exempla:
  "omens": {"on": "cursor { hide-after-inactive-ms 400; }", "off": ""}

  "omens": {"on": ["cursor {", "    hide-when-typing", "}"], "*": ""}

  "omens": {"*": {"tome": "visages/{{aspect}}.kdl", "illuminate": true}}

  // pattern Mark I, before conversion
  "scripture": {"tome": "visages/{{aspect}}.kdl", "illuminate": true}
