The rite's own scanning words, telling which aspect the machine bears

Some aspects leave no mark a step could read: a do-not-disturb raised from
a bar, a mode held only in the memory of a daemon. For such rites the
"auspex" is the rite's own scanning device — words spoken in the rite's
tongue that print the name of the aspect the machine stands in. What it
prints, trimmed of surrounding emptiness, is an omen like any other.

The auspex is written short, as the words alone, or long, as
{"rite": "<words>", "patience": "5s"}, where "rite" holds the words and
"patience" how long the auspex may labour; unless written, its patience is
two seconds. When its patience runs out its whole process group is slain.
An auspex that falls, outlasts its patience, or prints a word that names no
declared aspect gives no omen at all — it is never mistaken for an aspect.

The auspex is spoken in the rite's tongue (else the tongue of the settings,
else bash), from the hall of the rite's scripture, with the environment of
the rite's commands: SERVITOR_RITE, the inscriptions of the data-slate as
SERVITOR_INSCRIPTION_<KEY>, and SERVITOR_ASPECT. During an invocation
SERVITOR_ASPECT and {{aspect}} name the aspect being invoked; when an augury
or census asks it outside of one, they are empty. Placeholders may be spoken
in its words.

The augury, the census and the inquisition awaken the auspex. Completion
never does, so that a Tab is answered at once, and neither does --foresee,
which touches nothing at all. Where the auspex agrees with the omens of the
steps on an aspect the data-slate did not record, the rite is desecrated —
changed by other hands — which is noted, not condemned.

Kindred passages: omens, standing, augury, patience, tongue, aspects

Exempla:
  "auspex": "makoctl mode | grep -q do-not-disturb && echo on || echo off"

  "auspex": {"rite": "darkman get", "patience": "5s"}

  servitor augury do-not-disturb aspect
